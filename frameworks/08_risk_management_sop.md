# Risk Management Standard Operating Procedure

**Document ID:** VIB-FW-08
**Version:** 1.0
**Owner:** Director of Programmes
**Approved by:** Grants Committee
**Review cycle:** Annual

---

## 1. Purpose and Application

This SOP defines how risk is identified, scored, mitigated and reviewed on every funded project. A risk register is a **mandatory annexure** to every proposal and to every renewal application. A proposal without a scored register is returned unscored.

Risk management is proportionate. Tier 1 grants (under INR 10 lakh) require a register of at least **6 risks**; Tier 2 and above require at least **10 risks**, of which at least **3 must be contextual risks** from the taxonomy in Section 3 and at least **1 must be a safeguarding risk**.

Risk ownership sits with the partner's project lead. Portfolio risk sits with our programme manager. Risks scored **critical** are owned by the Director of Programmes.

---

## 2. Risk Register

### 2.1 Register Fields

Each entry must carry all nine fields:

1. Risk ID.
2. Risk category (from Section 3).
3. Risk statement, written as cause → event → consequence ("If teacher transfers exceed 20% mid-year, trained teachers leave, and training investment and classroom practice are lost").
4. Likelihood score (1–5).
5. Impact score (1–5).
6. Inherent risk score (likelihood × impact).
7. Mitigation actions, each with a named owner and a date.
8. Residual risk score after mitigation.
9. Trigger or early-warning indicator, and the escalation route.

A mitigation that is merely "will monitor closely" is not a mitigation.

### 2.2 Likelihood Scale

| Score | Label | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rare | Less than 10% chance of occurring during the grant period |
| 2 | Unlikely | 10–30% chance |
| 3 | Possible | 30–60% chance |
| 4 | Likely | 60–85% chance |
| 5 | Almost certain | Above 85%, or already occurring |

### 2.3 Impact Scale

| Score | Label | Financial | Delivery | Reputation / other |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Negligible | Under INR 50,000 | Under 2 weeks' delay | No external visibility |
| 2 | Minor | INR 50,000 – 2 lakh | 2–6 weeks' delay | Internal concern only |
| 3 | Moderate | INR 2–10 lakh | 6 weeks – 3 months' delay, or up to 15% of reach lost | Partner-level concern; a stakeholder complaint |
| 4 | Major | INR 10–50 lakh | 3–6 months' delay, or 15–40% of reach lost | Government relationship damaged; media interest |
| 5 | Severe | Above INR 50 lakh | Project unable to deliver, or above 40% of reach lost | Harm to a child; regulatory action; loss of FCRA or registration |

Any risk that could result in harm to a child is scored **impact 5**, without exception.

### 2.4 Risk Ratings and Required Response

| Score (L × I) | Rating | Required response |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | Low | Accept and monitor; review at each quarterly cycle |
| 5–9 | Moderate | Documented mitigation with an owner and a date; reviewed quarterly |
| 10–14 | High | Documented mitigation plus a contingency plan; reviewed **monthly**; reported to our programme manager in every quarterly report |
| 15–25 | Critical | Escalate to the Director of Programmes within **7 days**; written action plan within **14 days**; reported to the Board at its next meeting; may trigger suspension of disbursement |

A project carrying **three or more high risks**, or **any critical risk**, at appraisal requires Grants Committee sign-off before approval.

## 3. Contextual Risk: India Education Taxonomy

Registers must draw from this taxonomy. Generic corporate risk lists are not accepted.

**A. Government and political**

- A1 Transfer of the key officer (DEO, BEO, District Collector, State Project Director) — assume at least **one transfer per district per 18 months**.
- A2 Change of state government or of departmental priority.
- A3 Delay or refusal of NOC, MoU or SCERT approval (**Government Engagement Protocol, Section 2**).
- A4 Model Code of Conduct during elections freezing government engagement, typically for **45–75 days**.
- A5 Competing government campaigns (surveys, enumeration, election duty) consuming teacher time.
- A6 Withdrawal or re-prioritisation of Samagra Shiksha budget lines mid-year.

**B. School and academic**

- B1 Teacher vacancy and single-teacher schools.
- B2 Teacher transfer or deputation mid-year.
- B3 Loss of instructional days to examinations, festivals, weather and non-teaching duties — plan on no more than **200 usable days**.
- B4 Head teacher resistance or non-cooperation.
- B5 Multi-grade classrooms defeating level-based grouping.
- B6 Curriculum or textbook change mid-project.
- B7 Language of instruction differing from the child's home language.

**C. Community and child**

- C1 Child absenteeism and seasonal migration, particularly in agricultural and brick-kiln belts.
- C2 Cohort attrition above the **20%** threshold in **MEL Framework, Section 3.2**.
- C3 Girls' dropout at the upper-primary transition.
- C4 Exclusion of children with disabilities, and of SC/ST and first-generation learners.
- C5 Low SMC functionality or parental disengagement.

**D. Partner and delivery**

- D1 Field staff attrition — assume **20–30% annually** in rural field roles unless evidence shows otherwise.
- D2 Recruitment delay in remote blocks.
- D3 Over-extension: the project exceeding three times the partner's largest prior project (**Partner Due Diligence Policy, Section 2**).
- D4 Founder or key-person dependence.
- D5 Weak MIS and data-quality failure.
- D6 Sub-grantee non-performance.

**E. Financial and compliance**

- E1 Delay in tranche release due to late reporting.
- E2 Cost escalation above the assumed **5–7%** inflation.
- E3 Statutory lapse: FCRA renewal, 12A/80G validity, CSR-1, POSH annual report, TDS or PF default (**Compliance Policy, Section 2**).
- E4 Audit qualification or misappropriation.
- E5 Currency or donor-condition change for foreign-funded grants.

**F. Safeguarding, data and reputational**

- F1 Child protection incident (**Safeguarding Policy, Section 5 — Incident Reporting**) — impact always scored 5.
- F2 Corporal punishment by school staff during project hours.
- F3 Breach or misuse of child data (**Data Protection Policy, Section 5**).
- F4 Unauthorised photography or publication of child images.
- F5 Adverse media or social media incident involving the project or the department.

**G. External and environmental**

- G1 Extreme weather, flood or heat closing schools; assume **5–15 lost days** per year in vulnerable districts.
- G2 Epidemic or public-health closure.
- G3 Civil unrest or law-and-order restrictions.
- G4 Connectivity and electricity failure for technology-dependent components.

## 4. Risk Review

**Cadence**

| Review | Who | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Field risk check | Partner field supervisor | Monthly, with the MIS submission |
| Register update | Partner project lead | Quarterly, submitted with the quarterly report within **30 days** of quarter end |
| Joint review | Our programme manager and the partner project lead | Quarterly, minuted |
| High-risk review | Our programme manager | Monthly for any risk scored 10 or above |
| Portfolio risk review | Director of Programmes | Half-yearly, covering all grants |
| Board risk report | Director of Programmes to the Board | Half-yearly, plus immediate escalation of any critical risk |

**Rules**

- The register is a **living document**. A register resubmitted unchanged across two consecutive quarters is treated as not maintained and is raised as a finding.
- **New risks** must be added within **14 days** of identification.
- A **materialised risk** must be reported to our programme manager within **7 days**, or within **24 hours** where it is a safeguarding, fraud or child-harm event (**Safeguarding Policy, Section 5**; **Financial Policy, Section 8 — Assurance**).
- Closed risks are retained on the register, marked closed with the date and reason, not deleted.
- Every **fragile assumption** in **Programme Design Standards, Section 3.2 — Assumptions** must appear as a register entry.
- Where residual risk remains **high or critical** after mitigation, the project must state what it will do if the risk materialises — a contingency, not a hope.
- Risk performance is an input to renewal: a partner that failed to escalate a materialised risk is marked down under **Renewal Policy, Section 2.4 — Candour**.
- Portfolio-level risk concentration is monitored against the limits in **Strategy Note, Section 2 — Portfolio Structure**: no partner above **15%** and no state above **35%** of the annual grant budget.
