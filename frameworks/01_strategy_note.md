## 1. Mission and Scope

Our vision is **High-Quality Public Education for Every Child**. Our mission is to **improve learning levels in government schools by scaling proven solutions**. Our philosophy is **Educate, Empower, Enable**.

We are a grant-making organisation. We do not run schools or deliver classroom instruction ourselves. We fund implementing partners and we provide technical support to government education initiatives. Every grant we make must be able to answer one question: **how does this raise the measured learning level of a child in a government school?**

### 1.1 In Scope and Out of Scope

In scope:

- Foundational literacy and numeracy (FLN) for Grades 1–5, aligned to NIPUN Bharat goals.
- Remedial and catch-up learning for Grades 3–8 where children are two or more grade levels behind.
- Teacher and head-teacher professional development delivered through or alongside the government cadre (SCERT, DIET, BRC, CRC).
- Community and School Management Committee (SMC) demand-building where it is tied to a learning indicator.
- System strengthening: assessment design, data systems, academic supervision, and technical assistance to state and district education departments.

Out of scope, and normally refused at concept note stage (see **Funding Policy, Section 1**):

- Standalone infrastructure, mid-day meal, health or nutrition projects with no learning indicator.
- Private, low-fee-private or unaided school interventions.
- Individual child sponsorship.
- Enrolment or attendance projects where learning is assumed rather than measured.

At least **80% of the annual grant budget** must be committed to projects whose primary outcome indicator is a measured learning outcome. The remaining 20% may fund enabling work (data systems, capability building, research) that does not itself carry a learning indicator.

### 1.2 Public System Focus

We fund work that sits **inside the public education system**. A project is in scope only if it operates in government or government-aided schools, or inside government institutions (SCERT, DIET, block and cluster structures), or in anganwadis and government pre-primary centres feeding into government schools.

Requirements:

- **100% of directly served schools** must be government or government-aided. Exceptions require written Grants Committee approval and may not exceed **10% of the schools in any single project**.
- Every project must name its **government entry point**: the officer or institution (District Education Officer, BEO, BRC Coordinator, State Project Director, Samagra Shiksha) through whom access is obtained. See **Government Engagement Protocol, Section 1 — Stakeholder Mapping**.
- Parallel provision — after-school or out-of-school centres run independently of the school system — is funded only as a time-bound demonstration of at most **24 months**, and only where the proposal sets out how the model transfers into school hours or into the government cadre.
- Projects must use government data infrastructure where it exists: UDISE+ for school and enrolment data, state assessment systems for learning data, and NAS/ASER for benchmarking.

Working "with government" means working **through** government structures, not merely informing them. A letter of acknowledgement from a district office is not system engagement.

## 2. Portfolio Structure

The philosophy Educate, Empower, Enable maps to three funding portfolios. Every grant is assigned to exactly one primary portfolio at approval, and may name one secondary portfolio.

**Educate — direct learning gain.** Interventions that place a proven instructional method in front of children: teaching-at-the-right-level grouping, structured FLN instruction, remedial camps, reading programmes, print-rich classrooms, and learning materials tied to a curriculum. Success is measured as a change in child learning levels.

**Empower — the people around the child.** Teacher and head-teacher capability, academic mentoring by CRC and BRC coordinators, SMC and parent engagement, and adolescent agency programmes. Success is measured as a change in adult practice (classroom observation scores, mentoring visit quality) that is then linked to child learning.

**Enable — the system that carries the work.** Assessment and data systems, academic planning support to SCERT and DIET, teacher-education reform, governance and review mechanisms, and technical assistance placed inside a state department. Success is measured as institutional adoption: a policy issued, a cadre trained, a system running without our funding.

**Indicative annual allocation:** Educate 40%, Empower 35%, Enable 25%, with a tolerance of ±10 percentage points. Sustained drift beyond tolerance for two consecutive years must be reported to the Board with a rebalancing plan.

No single partner may hold more than **15% of the annual grant budget**. No single state may hold more than **35%**, so that the portfolio remains transferable across contexts.

## 3. Scaling Proven Solutions

We do not fund invention. We fund the movement of an approach that already works from where it works to where it does not.

A solution is treated as **proven** when at least one of the following holds:

- It has an external evaluation (randomised, quasi-experimental or a credible pre-post design with a comparison group) showing a learning effect of at least **0.15 standard deviations**, or an equivalent grade-level gain, published or documented in a report we can read.
- The applicant has run it for at least **two full academic years** with baseline and endline learning data on a minimum of **500 children**, showing improvement against a comparison or against a credible counterfactual.
- It is already adopted in a state government programme with published results.

Where the evidence bar is not met, the request is treated as a **pilot** and is capped at **INR 25 lakh over 12 months**, with a mandatory evidence review before any further funding. Pilots may not exceed **10% of the annual grant budget** in any year.

Applicants must cite their evidence with source, year, geography, sample size and effect size. See **Programme Design Standards, Section 2.4 — Evidence Base**.

## 4. Scaling Proven Solutions: The Scale Pathway and Stage-Gates

Every funded project sits at one of five stages. The stage determines the grant tier, the duration and the evidence required to move forward. Grant tiers are defined in **Funding Policy, Section 2** and used identically in **Partner Due Diligence Policy, Section 3** and **Financial Policy, Section 3**.

| Stage | Typical reach | Grant tier | Maximum duration | Gate to the next stage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Pilot | Up to 2,000 children; up to 20 schools | Under INR 10 lakh | 12 months | Baseline and endline on the same cohort; positive learning movement; unit cost within benchmark |
| 2. Proof of concept | 2,000–10,000 children; 20–100 schools | INR 10–50 lakh | 24 months | Effect of at least 0.15 SD, or two consecutive years of gain; retention of at least 80% of the cohort |
| 3. Demonstration (block) | 10,000–40,000 children; 100–500 schools | INR 50 lakh–2 crore | 36 months | Independent evaluation (mandatory above INR 50 lakh, see **MEL Framework, Section 6**); signed district or block MoU |
| 4. Scale-up (district/multi-district) | 40,000–200,000 children; 500+ schools | Above INR 2 crore | 48 months | Government cost-share of at least 25%; delivery through government cadre for at least 50% of contact hours |
| 5. Institutionalisation (state) | State cohort | Above INR 2 crore | 60 months, non-renewable | State order or budget line adopting the model; our funding falls below 25% of total programme cost |

Rules that apply across the pathway:

- A project may advance **one stage per funding cycle**. Skipping a stage requires Board approval.
- Total continuous funding to one partner for one model is capped at **five years**, after which either institutionalisation is demonstrated or the grant is exited under **Government Engagement Protocol, Section 5 — Exit**.
- Unit cost must fall as stage rises. We expect the cost per child at Stage 4 to be no more than **60% of the Stage 2 cost per child** for the same model. Benchmarks are in **Financial Policy, Section 3 — Value for Money**.
- Failure to meet a stage-gate does not end the relationship; it means re-funding at the same stage, once, for a maximum of one further cycle.
