**Owner:** Head of Partnerships

## 1. Scope and Eligibility

This policy governs applications for a further grant to a partner we already fund. Renewal is not automatic: it is appraised on the same standards as a new proposal, plus the evidence of what the previous grant achieved. Applications are due at least **90 days** before the current grant ends, on the standard renewal template.

**Eligibility gates.** A renewal is not considered unless all of the following hold:

1. All narrative and financial reports due under the current grant are submitted and accepted.
2. Utilisation is at least **75%** of funds disbursed at application, with the balance explained.
3. Activity completion is at least **80%** of the approved work plan (Section 2.3).
4. Reach is at least **85%** of target, or the shortfall is explained under Section 3.
5. Baseline and at least one further round of learning assessment are complete (**MEL Framework, Section 3.3**).
6. No unresolved safeguarding, fraud or compliance matter is outstanding.
7. Due diligence has been refreshed within **12 months** (**Partner Due Diligence Policy, Section 0**).
8. Total continuous funding for this model will not exceed **five years** on completion of the proposed phase (**Funding Policy, Section 2**).

Where a stage-gate in **Strategy Note, Section 4** has not been met, renewal is possible **once** at the same stage, for one further cycle.

## 2. Performance Reporting

### 2.1 Results Against the Framework

The renewal must report **every indicator** in the approved results framework — baseline, target, actual, variance — for each year. Indicators may not be dropped, redefined or replaced; where one was changed with approval, cite the approval date.

Learning results must state the assessment tool, date, sample size, sampling method, cohort attrition and disaggregation by sex and grade (**MEL Framework, Section 4 — Outcome Reporting**).

### 2.2 Reach

Reach must be reported as numbers, not adjectives, in three categories: **enrolled** (children registered); **reached** (attended at least **60%** of sessions — the headline figure and the denominator for cost per child); and **assessed** (completed both baseline and endline).

Also required:

- Counts of schools, teachers, head teachers, CRC/BRC coordinators and SMC members engaged.
- **Disaggregation** by sex, grade, and where targeted, social category and disability status.
- **Cumulative versus annual** figures labelled. A child served in two years counts **once** cumulatively.
- Reach **against target** as a percentage. Delivery below **85% of target** must be explained in Section 3.
- Indirect beneficiaries may be reported separately but **not** added to headline reach.
- Geographic coverage against plan: districts, blocks and schools covered versus proposed.

### 2.3 Activity Completion

A completion table against the approved work plan, activity by activity: planned quantity, delivered quantity, percentage complete, and reason for any gap.

- Overall completion below **80%** requires a written explanation and normally blocks renewal (Section 1); completion between **80% and 95%** requires an explanation for each incomplete activity.
- Training reports **days delivered per teacher**, attendance rate, and follow-up visit rate against the standard of one on-site visit per teacher per month (**Programme Design Standards, Section 3.3**).
- Delivery is reported against **usable instructional days** actually available, with the number stated.
- Where an activity was dropped, say when the decision was taken, by whom, and whether prior approval was obtained (**MEL Framework, Section 5**).

### 2.4 Candour

Candour is scored. A renewal that reports only success is treated as incomplete.

- State at least **three things that did not work** and what was learned from each.
- Report **missed targets in the same detail as met targets**, with reason and corrective action.
- Report **null or negative results** where assessment found no gain.
- Disclose every **materialised risk** and whether it was escalated within the required window (**Risk Management SOP, Section 4 — Risk Review**).
- Disclose all **safeguarding incidents, allegations and investigations**, substantiated or not (**Safeguarding Policy, Section 5 — Incident Reporting**).
- Disclose **key-role staff departures**, any **audit qualification** and any **statutory lapse** (**Compliance Policy, Section 3**), and any **complaint** through the grievance channel (**Programme Design Standards, Section 3.6**).

Concealment discovered after approval is a material breach. A partner that reported a failure honestly and acted on it scores **above** a partner reporting no failures at all.

## 3. Variance Analysis

Variance analysis explains the gap between plan and reality. Each dimension requires a stated cause, an evidenced consequence and a corrective action.

**3.1 Reach variance.** Any deviation beyond **±15%** of target reach must be explained. Over-delivery is explained too, since it usually signals thinner delivery per child.

**3.2 Budget variance**, reported line by line against the approved budget:

- Variance up to **10%** per line needs no prior approval but must be explained where it exceeds **INR 1 lakh**.
- Variance above **10%** must cite the prior written approval obtained; where none was obtained, say so.
- Variance above **20%**, or any movement into or out of overhead, must cite Grants Committee approval (**Financial Policy, Section 7.2 — Variance**).
- Overhead charged must be stated as a percentage of direct costs and must not exceed **15%**, or **20%** where a written Board exception was granted (**Financial Policy, Section 4 — Overhead Limits**).

**3.3 Timeline variance.** Activities delayed more than **one quarter** must be explained, with the knock-on effect on outcomes.

**3.4 Cost variance.** Actual cost per child, computed on children **reached**, against the proposal figure and the benchmark range in **Financial Policy, Section 3 — Value for Money**. A rise of more than **25%** requires explanation; a cost above the benchmark range requires the approval trail in **Funding Policy, Section 4.1**.

**3.5 Outcome variance.** Where learning gains fell short, distinguish design failure, delivery failure and measurement problems, and say which. "External circumstances" alone is not an analysis.

## 4. Forward Design

The renewal must set out the next phase as a designed project, not a continuation request.

- **Updated evidence of need** for the next-phase geography, data no older than **three years** (**Programme Design Standards, Section 2.1**).
- **Revised theory of change**, marking what has changed and why, based on what the previous phase showed.
- **Design changes** responding to the learning in Section 2.4, each traceable to a specific finding.
- **Refreshed due diligence** pack: updated audited accounts, registrations and disclosures (**Partner Due Diligence Policy, Sections 1, 3 and 6**).
- **Updated risk register**, materialised risks marked and new risks added (**Risk Management SOP, Section 2**).
- **Updated government position**: MoU or NOC status, changes in key offices, and the institutionalisation marker reached (**Government Engagement Protocol, Section 4**).
- **Updated staffing plan** against the ratios in **Programme Management SOP, Section 4.3**.
- **Safeguarding and compliance update**, including the annual declaration and the POSH filing date.
- **Path to exit**: what government will own, what it will cost the state, and by when.

A renewal proposing the identical design with a larger number attached will be marked down.

## 5. Next Phase Targets

### 5.1 Target Setting

- Every indicator carries a **baseline (the previous phase endline), annual milestones and an end-of-phase target**.
- Targets must be **evidence-based**, derived from the achieved rate of change, not an aspiration. A target implying more than **1.5 times** the achieved rate must be justified.
- At least **three outcome indicators**, per **MEL Framework, Section 2.3 — Output vs Outcome**.
- Next-phase cost per child must be stated and must fall where scale increases — at Stage 4, no more than **60%** of the Stage 2 cost per child for the same model (**Strategy Note, Section 4**).
- Reach targets must be consistent with the staffing plan and the budget.

### 5.2 Continuity and Change

State plainly what continues and what changes, under three headings: **Continue** — components retained, with the evidence they worked; **Change** — components modified, with the finding that prompted it and the expected effect; **Stop** — components discontinued, with the reason. A renewal that stops nothing after a full phase of learning is treated as not having learned.

Also required:

- **Geographic continuity**: whether the next phase deepens in the same blocks, expands to new blocks, or both, with the budget and reach split between the two.
- **Cohort continuity**: whether the same children continue or a new cohort begins, and how the previous cohort's gains are sustained.
- **Staffing continuity**: retention of the Project Lead and key roles, and the replacement plan where they have left.
- **Government continuity**: ownership moved one step further along the institutionalisation ladder (**Government Engagement Protocol, Section 4**).

Where the next phase differs from the previous by more than **40%** of budget or geography, it is appraised as a **new proposal**, not a renewal.

### 5.3 Duration

- Durations follow the tiers in **Funding Policy, Section 2**: Tier 1 up to 12 months, Tier 2 up to 24, Tier 3 up to 36, Tier 4 up to 48, extendable to 60 months only for institutionalisation.
- Total continuous funding to one partner for one model may not exceed **five years**; the renewal must state cumulative years funded to date.
- A final phase must be designated as such and carry an **exit plan**, due no later than the mid-point of the final grant year (**Government Engagement Protocol, Section 5 — Exit**).
- **No-cost extensions** up to **6 months** may be approved by the Director of Programmes, beyond that by the Grants Committee. They do not extend the five-year cap.
- Bridge funding between phases is limited to **6 months** and **20%** of the previous annual grant value.
- The renewal decision is communicated at least **30 days** before the current grant ends, provided the application arrived on time.
