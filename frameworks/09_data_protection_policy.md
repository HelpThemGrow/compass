## 1. Scope and Legal Basis

This policy governs all personal data processed in connection with a funded project: data about children, parents and guardians, teachers, head teachers, government officials, partner staff, volunteers and donors. It applies to us, every partner and sub-grantee, and every consultant, evaluator, enumerator or contractor who touches project data. Compliance is a condition of the grant agreement.

Governing law: the **Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 (DPDP Act)**, the primary statute and the source of the children's-data obligations in Section 4; the **Information Technology Act 2000**, Section 43A and the SPDI Rules 2011; **POCSO Act 2012, Section 23**, prohibiting disclosure of the identity of a child involved in an offence; the **Right to Information Act 2005** where government-held data is involved; and the **Juvenile Justice Act 2015** for children in institutional care.

Data collected under a government data-sharing agreement is additionally subject to that agreement (**Government Engagement Protocol, Section 2 — Permissions**).

## 2. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Held by | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Data Fiduciary | Our organisation, and the partner for data it collects | Determines purpose and means; accountable for lawful processing |
| Data Processor | Evaluators, MIS vendors, printing and survey agencies | Processes on documented instruction only, under a written contract |
| **Data Protection Officer (DPO)** | Named senior role in our organisation | Owns this policy; approves data-sharing agreements; leads breach response |
| **Partner Data Custodian** | Named role in each partner, notified at grant signing | Day-to-day control of project data; maintains the data inventory |
| Designated Safeguarding Lead | See **Safeguarding Policy** | Controls access to incident and child-protection records |

Requirements:

- Every partner names a **Data Custodian in writing within 15 days** of grant signing.
- Every processor engagement requires a written **data processing agreement** before data is shared, specifying purpose, retention, security, sub-processing, breach notification within **24 hours**, and deletion on termination.
- A **data inventory** — every dataset, its fields, lawful basis, location and retention date — is maintained by each partner and reviewed **annually**.
- Every staff member with data access completes **data protection training at induction and annually thereafter (minimum 2 hours)**.

## 3. General Principles

All processing must satisfy the following. Each is auditable.

1. **Lawful and consented.** Personal data is processed only with valid consent or another lawful basis under the DPDP Act. Consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, given by clear affirmative action, and available in the data principal's language.
2. **Purpose limitation.** Data collected for programme monitoring may not be reused for fundraising, marketing, advocacy publications or research without fresh consent.
3. **Data minimisation.** Collect only what an indicator in the results framework requires. Aadhaar numbers, caste certificates, religion, health records, family bank details and household income are **not collected** unless a specific legal requirement is documented and approved in writing by the DPO.
4. **Accuracy.** Data is corrected within **30 days** of a correction request.
5. **Storage limitation.** Data is retained only for the periods below and in Section 4.5, then deleted or irreversibly anonymised.
6. **Security.** Encryption in transit (TLS) and at rest for any file with identifiable child data; password-protected devices; no personal data on personal devices, personal email, consumer messaging apps or unmanaged cloud storage; access logs kept **12 months**; paper records in locked storage.
7. **Retention schedule.**

| Record type | Retention period |
|---|---|
| Identifiable child data | Maximum **3 years** after project close, then anonymised or deleted |
| Consent records | **7 years** |
| Anonymised or aggregated datasets | Indefinite |
| Financial and grant records | **8 years** (**Financial Policy, Section 7.1**) |
| FCRA records | **6 years** (**Compliance Policy, Section 2**) |
| Staff screening records | Employment plus **3 years** (**Safeguarding Policy, Section 3**) |
| Safeguarding incident records | **7 years** after closure, or until the youngest child concerned reaches 25, whichever is later |
| Access and audit logs | **12 months** |

8. **Rights.** Data principals may request access, correction, erasure and withdrawal of consent; requests are acknowledged within **7 days** and answered within **30 days**. A published grievance mechanism is required; unresolved grievances may be escalated to the Data Protection Board of India.
9. **Localisation.** Data is stored on servers within India by default. Transfer outside India requires written DPO approval and a contractual safeguard.

## 4. Children's Data

This section overrides any conflicting practice. A **child** is any person under **18 years**.

### 4.1 Lawful Basis and Guardian Consent

- Under the **DPDP Act 2023, Section 9**, a child's personal data may be processed only after **verifiable consent from the parent or lawful guardian**.
- Consent must be in **writing**, in the language the guardian reads or has read to them, on a form stating who is collecting, what fields, for what purpose, how long it is kept, with whom it may be shared, and how to withdraw.
- **Verification of the guardian relationship** is recorded (school record; identity document sighted but not copied or retained).
- Where a school or department shares data with a partner, the partner must still confirm a lawful basis exists; a government instruction does not substitute for the guardian consent required by Section 9.
- **Assent from the child** is sought in age-appropriate language for photography, filming, interview or case-study documentation.
- **Learning assessment** conducted as routine school academic activity under the head teacher's authority may rely on the school's own basis, provided results are held at aggregate level. Retaining named child assessment records requires guardian consent.
- Consent is recorded in a **consent register** with date, guardian name, child code, scope and expiry, retained **7 years**.
- Consent may be withdrawn at any time; data is removed from active use within **30 days**.

### 4.2 Prohibitions

The DPDP Act prohibits, and this policy enforces absolutely:

- **Tracking or behavioural monitoring of children**, and **targeted advertising** directed at children.
- Processing likely to cause any **detrimental effect on the well-being of a child**.
- **Sharing identifiable child data with any third party** — donors, corporate CSR funders, media, researchers, other NGOs, technology vendors — except where guardian consent explicitly covers that recipient, or disclosure is required by law or by a child-protection authority. **There is no exception for donor reporting**; donors receive aggregated or anonymised data only.
- Publishing a child's name, photograph, school, village or any identifying combination in any report, website, social media post, proposal or presentation, without specific written guardian consent for that use.
- Disclosing the identity of a child involved in a safeguarding incident (**POCSO Section 23**).
- Storing child data on personal devices, personal email, consumer messaging groups or public cloud folders.
- Collecting Aadhaar numbers of children.

### 4.3 Anonymisation

- Every child record carries a **pseudonymous child code**. The linking key is held in a **separate, encrypted file** accessible only to the Partner Data Custodian and one named deputy.
- Any dataset leaving the partner's control — analysis, evaluator or otherwise — must be **de-identified**: no name, parent name, address, photograph, contact number or Aadhaar.
- Aggregate reporting must **suppress any cell containing fewer than 5 children** to prevent re-identification.
- The Data Custodian verifies anonymisation before any external release and records the check.

### 4.4 Access Control

- Access to identifiable child data is **need-to-know only**, granted in writing by the Data Custodian, with a named list reviewed **quarterly**.
- Access to the linking key is capped at **two people per project**.
- Access is revoked within **48 hours** of a staff member leaving or changing role.
- Every access is logged; logs are retained **12 months** and audited **twice a year**.
- Evaluators and researchers receive de-identified data only, under written agreement, and may not attempt re-identification.
- Photographs and video of children sit in a separate access-controlled folder with the consent reference recorded against each file.

### 4.5 Retention and Deletion

- Identifiable child data is deleted or irreversibly anonymised no later than **3 years after project close**.
- Deletion is **documented** — dataset, date, method, person — and countersigned by the Data Custodian.
- Backups holding child data are purged on the same schedule; backup retention may not exceed **12 months**.
- On closure or termination, the partner certifies deletion or anonymisation in the handover pack within **90 days** (**Government Engagement Protocol, Section 5 — Exit**).

## 5. Breach Response

A breach is any unauthorised access, disclosure, alteration, loss or destruction of personal data — a lost phone or laptop, a misdirected email, an unsecured spreadsheet, an unauthorised photograph shared onward.

| Step | Timeline |
|---|---|
| Report to the Partner Data Custodian | **Immediately**, within **6 hours** of discovery |
| Notify our Data Protection Officer in writing | Within **24 hours** of discovery |
| Contain: revoke access, isolate systems, recover or delete exposed data | Within **24 hours** |
| Notify the **Data Protection Board of India** where required under the DPDP Act | Without delay, and in all cases within **72 hours** |
| Notify affected data principals (guardians) where there is a risk of harm | Within **72 hours** |
| Interim written report to the DPO | Within **72 hours** |
| Full investigation report with root cause and corrective actions | Within **30 days** |
| Corrective actions implemented | Within **60 days** |
| Board notification for any breach involving child data | At the next Board meeting, and immediately where more than **100 children** are affected |

Additional rules:

- Any breach involving child data is **severe (impact 5)** in the risk register (**Risk Management SOP, Section 2.3**).
- Where the breach also discloses a safeguarding concern, **Safeguarding Policy, Section 5** applies in parallel and takes precedence.
- Concealment of a breach is a material breach of the grant agreement and may suspend disbursement and trigger recovery of funds.
- All breaches, including near misses, are recorded in a **breach register** reviewed annually by the DPO, with aggregate statistics to the Board **half-yearly**.
