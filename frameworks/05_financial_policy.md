# Financial Policy for Grants

**Document ID:** VIB-FW-05
**Version:** 1.0
**Owner:** Director of Finance
**Approved by:** Board of Trustees
**Review cycle:** Annual

---

## 1. The Ask

Every proposal must state, on the first page: the **total amount requested** in Indian Rupees in figures and words, using lakh and crore consistently; the **grant period** in months with start and end dates aligned to the academic year where possible; the **year-wise split** for multi-year grants; the **total project cost** where it differs, and the source of the balance; the **direct beneficiary numbers** used to derive unit cost; and the **cost per child per year** (total project cost ÷ direct beneficiaries ÷ project years).

The requested amount must match the budget total exactly; a mismatch is a blocking error. Amounts must be consistent across the proposal, the budget sheet and the results framework.

---

## 2. Budget Format

Budgets are submitted on our template as a spreadsheet annexure, with a summary table in the proposal body. Every budget uses these seven heads:

1. **Personnel** — position, posts, monthly gross cost, months charged, percentage charged to this grant.
2. **Programme delivery** — training, materials, assessments, events, learning camps.
3. **Travel and field costs**, with the rate basis stated.
4. **Monitoring, evaluation and learning**, including external evaluation where mandatory.
5. **Capital and equipment**, itemised with justification.
6. **Administrative overhead** — subject to **Section 4**.
7. **Contingency** — capped at **3% of direct costs**.

Rules:

- Every line shows **unit, quantity, unit rate and total**. A single figure against "Training — INR 8,00,000" is rejected.
- Costs are shown **year by year** for multi-year grants.
- Capital and equipment may not exceed **10% of total grant value**. Land or building purchase is never funded (**Funding Policy, Section 1**).
- Any single salary above **INR 15 lakh per annum** charged to the grant requires written justification and Grants Committee approval.
- Statutory employer costs (PF, ESI, gratuity provision) must be shown, not omitted.
- GST and TDS treatment stated; amounts are inclusive of taxes unless stated otherwise.
- Co-funding and in-kind contributions (school space, teacher time, textbooks) are shown in a separate column and valued, but may not be counted toward our disbursement.

## 3. Value for Money

Value for money is assessed as economy, efficiency and effectiveness, expressed primarily as **cost per child per year**. The benchmark ranges below (INR per direct beneficiary child per year) are used in all appraisals and are the ranges cited in **Strategy Note, Section 4** and **Renewal Policy, Section 3 — Variance Analysis**.

| Intervention type | Benchmark range (INR per child per year) |
|---|---|
| System strengthening / technical assistance to state or district | 40 – 200 |
| Teacher professional development delivered through the government cadre | 150 – 600 |
| In-school FLN or direct instruction (project-supported facilitator) | 800 – 1,800 |
| Remedial or learning-camp models (time-bound, intensive) | 400 – 1,200 |
| EdTech or blended learning (device-light) | 300 – 900 |
| Community and SMC mobilisation | 100 – 400 |
| Residential or intensive bridge programmes | 12,000 – 30,000 |

Application:

- A proposal outside the relevant range must justify it in the budget narrative. Costs more than **25% above** the top of the range require Grants Committee approval; more than **50% above** requires Board approval.
- Costs **below** the range are scrutinised too: an implausibly low unit cost usually indicates inflated reach or under-costed delivery.
- Unit cost is computed on **children reached** (attended at least 60% of sessions) per **MEL Framework, Section 4 — Outcome Reporting**, not on children enrolled or indirect beneficiaries.
- Cost per child should fall as the project scales (**Strategy Note, Section 4**).
- Proposals must state the cost per child and show the arithmetic.

## 4. Overhead Limits

**The administrative overhead ceiling is 15% of total direct project costs.**

- Overhead may not exceed **15%**. An exception up to a maximum of **20%** may be approved only by the **Board, in writing, with reasons minuted**. No exception above 20% is available under any circumstance.
- This ceiling is identical to the organisational administrative cost ratio ceiling in **Partner Due Diligence Policy, Section 3 — Financial Health**.
- Overhead includes head office rent and utilities, senior management time not directly charged, finance and HR staff, statutory audit, general insurance, IT and communications, and governance costs.
- Overhead **excludes** (these are direct costs): project staff salaries, field office costs, MEL costs (3–7% per **MEL Framework, Section 1**), external evaluation, and project-specific training and materials.
- Where a grant is sub-granted, the **combined** overhead of lead and sub-grantees may not exceed 15% of direct costs, and the lead partner's own share may not exceed **10%**.
- Overhead is recovered pro rata with direct expenditure; it cannot be drawn in full in the first tranche.
- Overhead is calculated on **direct costs excluding capital items** above INR 1 lakh and excluding pass-through sub-grants.

## 5. Budget Narrative

The narrative explains the numbers. It is required and is scored separately. It must contain:

- Justification for each of the seven heads, and specifically for any head above **20% of the total**.
- The **basis of each rate**: salary benchmarks, printing quotations, travel rates (state government rates or actuals), venue and per-diem rates.
- **Assumptions**: inflation year on year (state the percentage; we expect **5–7%**), attrition and replacement costs, and working months per post.
- The **overhead calculation** as a percentage of direct costs, with components listed.
- The **cost per child** calculation with numerator and denominator.
- Any **cost driver** making the project dearer than benchmark: terrain, remote or tribal blocks, multi-lingual materials, low school density.
- **Co-funding** from other funders and government, in cash and in kind, and a statement that no cost here is claimed from another funder.

## 6. Disbursement

Funds are released in tranches against a signed grant agreement and satisfactory reporting.

| Grant size | Tranche structure |
|---|---|
| Under INR 10 lakh | 60% on signing, 40% on satisfactory completion report |
| INR 10–50 lakh | 40% / 40% / 20% at signing, mid-term and final |
| INR 50 lakh – 2 crore | 30% / 30% / 30% / 10%, released half-yearly, final tranche on audited utilisation |
| Above INR 2 crore | Annual tranches with half-yearly sub-releases; final 10% retained until the independent evaluation report is accepted |

Rules:

- The first tranche is released within **15 working days** of a fully signed agreement and receipt of the partner's bank mandate.
- A subsequent tranche is released only when at least **75% of the previous tranche** is utilised and reported, and the narrative and financial reports are accepted.
- Release is processed within **20 working days** of an accepted utilisation report.
- Disbursement is withheld where: the baseline is not completed within 90 days (**MEL Framework, Section 3.3**), a safeguarding incident is unreported (**Safeguarding Policy, Section 5**), a statutory registration has lapsed (**Compliance Policy, Section 2**), or an unexplained variance above 10% is outstanding.
- Funds are received in the partner's designated project account; foreign contribution only in the designated FCRA account.
- Interest earned is reported and either adjusted against the next tranche or refunded at close.
- Unspent funds are refunded within **60 days** of the final report, unless a written no-cost extension is approved.

## 7. Utilisation Reporting

### 7.1 Reporting Cadence and Content

- **Quarterly utilisation statement** within **30 days** of quarter end, showing budget, quarterly and cumulative expenditure, balance and percentage utilisation by line item.
- **Annual utilisation certificate (UC)**, signed by the Chief Executive and a chartered accountant, within **90 days** of the financial year end.
- **Final UC and audited project statement** within **90 days** of grant close.
- Expenditure is reported against the **same line items** as the approved budget; restructured formats are returned.
- Supporting documents are retained **eight years** and produced on request within **10 working days**.

### 7.2 Variance

**The 10% rule.** A partner may reallocate up to **10% of any budget line** without prior approval, provided the total grant value is unchanged and no new budget head is created.

- Cumulative variance on a line above **10%** requires **prior written approval** from the programme manager, obtained before the expenditure is incurred.
- Variance above **20%** on any line, or any reallocation into or out of overhead, requires **Grants Committee approval**.
- Any variance above **INR 1 lakh** in absolute terms must be explained in the utilisation report, whatever its percentage.
- **Underspend** above 10% at half-year requires a catch-up plan; persistent underspend above 25% at year end may reduce the next tranche.
- **Reach** variance is treated the same way: delivery below **85% of target reach** must be explained under **Renewal Policy, Section 3 — Variance Analysis**.
- Retrospective approval is granted only by exception and is recorded.

## 8. Assurance

- **Statutory audit** of the partner organisation annually, by a chartered accountant.
- **Project audit certificate** for every grant above **INR 10 lakh**, covering receipts, expenditure by line item and confirmation of purpose; a **separate project audit report** above **INR 50 lakh**, by a chartered accountant who is not a related party.
- **Auditor rotation**: the same firm may not sign the partner's accounts for more than **five consecutive years**.
- **Our right of audit**: at any time during the grant and for **three years** after close, with **15 days' notice**, or immediately where fraud is suspected. **Spot verification** of vouchers, bank statements, registers and assets with **48 hours' notice**.
- **Fraud response**: suspected misappropriation reported to the Director of Finance within **24 hours**; disbursement suspended immediately; investigation completed within **45 days**; confirmed misuse recovered in full within **90 days** and reported to the Board.
- Assurance findings inform the next due diligence refresh under **Partner Due Diligence Policy, Section 0**.
