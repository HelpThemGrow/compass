# Concept Note Template

**Document ID:** VIB-TPL-03
**Version:** 1.0
**Owner:** Head of Partnerships
**Approved by:** Grants Committee
**Review cycle:** Annual

A concept note is a screening document, not a proposal. Keep it to 3–5 pages. Complete every section; do not delete a heading. We respond within 15 working days. A full proposal is invited only if the note passes screening.

---

## Summary

Five to eight sentences: the problem, your approach, where you will work, how many children you will reach, the amount you are asking for, over what period, and the learning outcome you expect to change. Include a named contact with email and phone number.

## Problem

Quantify the learning deficit in your target districts and blocks with at least two sourced figures (ASER, NAS, UDISE+, state assessment or your own baseline), and say briefly why the problem persists despite existing government provision.

## Proposed Approach

Describe what you will actually do — who delivers it, to whom, how often and for how long — and cite the evidence that this approach has raised learning levels before, with geography, sample size and effect.

## Expected Outcomes

State two to four outcomes as measurable changes in children, teachers or the system, with an indicative target figure and how you would measure each.

## Scale and Geography

Give state, districts and blocks, the number of government or government-aided schools, the grade range, and the number of children and teachers directly reached.

## About the Organisation

Registration type, number and year; 12A, 80G, FCRA and CSR-1 status; annual turnover; number of staff; and two comparable prior projects in government schools with their results.

## Indicative Budget

Total amount requested in figures and words, the split by year, the total project cost if different, and the cost per child per year with the arithmetic shown.

## Duration

Number of months, proposed start and end dates aligned to the academic year, and the phase this represents — pilot, proof of concept, demonstration, scale-up or institutionalisation.
