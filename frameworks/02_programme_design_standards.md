# Programme Design Standards

**Document ID:** VIB-FW-02
**Version:** 1.0
**Owner:** Head of Programme Design
**Approved by:** Grants Committee
**Review cycle:** Annual

---

## 1. Purpose and Application

These standards define what a fundable programme design contains, and apply to every concept note, full proposal and renewal. Where a standard cannot be met, say so and explain why. A proposal failing **two or more** mandatory elements in Sections 2 and 3 is returned without scoring.

---

## 2. Evidence of Need

### 2.1 Evidence of Need

The problem must be quantified for the **exact geography of the project**, not for India or the state in general. Mandatory elements:

- At least **three** numeric statements of the learning deficit, each with a named source and year (ASER 2024, NAS 2021, UDISE+ 2023-24, a state FLN assessment, or the partner's own baseline).
- The source must be no older than **three years** at submission. Older data may be used only if it is the most recent available and this is stated.
- A comparison against the state average and, where available, the national average.
- The unit of the statistic must be explicit: percentage of children in Grade 3 who can read a Grade 2 text, not "poor reading levels".

Where no district-level data exists, the applicant must present a **baseline sample of at least 200 children** from the target schools, with the assessment tool named.

### 2.2 Targeting

State, as numbers:

- State, district and block; cluster or village lists in the annexures.
- Number of schools, split by government and government-aided.
- Grade range and children per grade.
- Total direct beneficiaries (children), and separately, indirect beneficiaries. Indirect counts may not be added into headline reach. See **MEL Framework, Section 4 — Outcome Reporting**.
- Number of teachers, head teachers, CRC/BRC coordinators and SMC members engaged.
- Selection criteria for schools: UDISE+ enrolment, prior assessment scores, distance from block headquarters, proportion of SC/ST or first-generation learners.

Reach must be consistent with the budget and staffing plan, and is checked against the supervision ratios in **Programme Management SOP, Section 4 — Operating Systems**.

### 2.3 Gap Analysis and Root Cause

**Gap analysis.** The proposal must map what already exists before claiming a gap: the relevant Samagra Shiksha components and their coverage; state FLN mission (NIPUN Bharat) activity including any teacher training cascade; other NGO or CSR programmes in the same blocks, named where known; and existing DIET and BRC academic support. It must then state the **residual gap** in one or two sentences: what will still be missing after existing provision is counted.

**Root cause.** The proposal must argue *why* the deficit persists despite existing provision, and distinguish causes from symptoms. "Children cannot read" is a symptom. Acceptable root-cause statements name a mechanism: curriculum pace outrunning the class, no grouping by learning level, teachers untrained in decoding instruction, academic supervision reduced to administrative inspection, teacher vacancy, multi-grade classrooms without materials.

At least **two root causes** must be identified, and each activity in Section 3.3 must be traceable to one of them. A root cause with no corresponding activity, or an activity with no corresponding root cause, is a design fault.

### 2.4 Evidence Base

The approach must have worked before, consistent with **Strategy Note, Section 3 — Scaling Proven Solutions**.

Each citation must give: study or programme name, organisation, geography, year, sample size, design and effect found (standard deviations, percentage points or grade-level equivalents). At least **two** independent citations are expected for Tier 3 and Tier 4 grants (INR 50 lakh and above).

Where the applicant's own results are the evidence, baseline and endline must be on the same cohort with attrition stated; attrition above **25%** makes them indicative only. Contrary evidence should be acknowledged.

## 3. Programme Logic and Design

### 3.1 Theory of Change

A complete theory of change has **five levels**: inputs, activities, outputs, outcomes, impact. Each level must be present and each link argued, not asserted.

- The activity-to-outcome statement must name a mechanism ("teachers group children by assessed level, so instruction meets the child's actual level, so decoding improves within 60 teaching days").
- Outcomes are changes in children, teachers or the system — not completed activities.
- State the time lag between activity and outcome. A claim of learning gains within a single term of a 90-hour intervention must be justified.
- A one-page diagram is required as an annexure; the narrative must stand alone without it.

### 3.2 Assumptions

Assumptions are the conditions outside the project's control on which the logic depends. The proposal must list at least **five** and mark which are **fragile** (plausible but not assured): school days actually available after examinations, elections and festivals; teacher transfer rates; continuity of the officer who granted access; teacher availability for training on non-teaching days; parental willingness to send children to remedial sessions; timely receipt of government data.

Every fragile assumption must appear in the risk register with a mitigation. See **Risk Management SOP, Section 2 — Risk Register**.

### 3.3 Activity Specification

Each major activity must carry **five attributes**: who does it, to whom, how often, for how long, and where.

Acceptable: "One trained facilitator per school runs a 60-minute FLN session, 4 days per week, for 24 weeks, in the Grade 2–3 classroom during the first period." Unacceptable: "Regular remedial support will be provided."

- Annual contact hours per child must be calculable. For direct-instruction models we expect a minimum of **80 contact hours per child per year**; below this, justify the expected effect.
- Teacher training must state days per teacher per year (minimum **6 days** for a new model), the trainer-to-participant ratio (maximum **1:30**), and the follow-up cadence (minimum **one on-site visit per teacher per month** in year one).
- Materials must be listed with quantity per child and unit cost, feeding the budget under **Financial Policy, Section 2 — Budget Format**.

### 3.4 Sequencing

Activities must be laid out against the **Indian academic calendar**, not a generic 12-month grid.

- Show months against the academic year (typically April/June to March), excluding summer vacation, examination weeks and known non-instructional periods.
- Assume no more than **200 usable instructional days** per academic year, and state the figure used.
- Schedule preparatory activities — MoU or NOC, recruitment, printing, baseline — before the first child-facing activity. Allow at least **8 weeks** for government permissions (**Government Engagement Protocol, Section 2 — Permissions**) and **6 weeks** for recruitment.
- Complete baseline assessment before the intervention starts, within the first **60 days** of the grant.
- State dependencies: which activities cannot start until another finishes.

### 3.5 System Fit

The design must show how the intervention sits within the government system rather than beside it.

- Name the government structures used — SCERT, DIET, BEO, BRC, CRC, SMC — and the role each plays.
- State the proportion of delivery carried by government personnel versus project staff, and how it changes over the grant. By the final year of a Stage 3 or higher project (**Strategy Note, Section 4**), at least **50%** of contact hours should be delivered by government cadre.
- Show alignment to NIPUN Bharat goals, the state FLN mission and the relevant Samagra Shiksha annual work plan and budget (AWP&B) line.
- State how project data will flow into government systems (UDISE+, state MIS) rather than a parallel database only.
- Confirm no cash payments, honoraria or salary top-ups to serving government staff. Reimbursement of actual travel and per-diem at government rates is permitted and must appear in the budget.

### 3.6 Participation

Design must be informed by the people it affects, and must plan for their continued voice.

- Describe the design consultation: how many teachers, parents, SMC members and children were consulted, when, and what changed as a result. Minimum **20 teachers and 3 SMC meetings** for a district-level proposal.
- Set out the ongoing feedback mechanism: at least **one structured teacher feedback round per term** and **two SMC review meetings per year** per cluster, with a named person responsible for acting on it.
- Include a grievance channel for parents and children with a response time of **15 working days**, kept separate from the safeguarding route in **Safeguarding Policy, Section 4 — Reporting**.
- Address equity explicitly: how girls, children with disabilities, out-of-school and first-generation learners, and children from SC/ST and migrant households will be included, with disaggregated targets.
