## 0. Scope and Principle

This policy applies to every person funded by us or acting on our behalf: our staff, board members, consultants, volunteers, partner organisations, their staff and volunteers, sub-grantees, contractors, evaluators, photographers and visitors to project sites.

The guiding principle is **zero tolerance of harm to children and zero tolerance of inaction**. The safety of the child overrides organisational reputation, partner relationships and grant continuity in every case.

Statutory framework: the **Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act 2012**, the **Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act 2015**, the **Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act 2009 (Section 17 — prohibition of physical punishment and mental harassment)**, the **Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act 2013 (POSH)**, and the **Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023** for children's data (see **Data Protection Policy, Section 4 — Children's Data**).

## 1. Partner Requirements and Ongoing Compliance

A grant may not be signed until the partner meets every requirement below. Compliance is verified at due diligence (**Partner Due Diligence Policy, Section 4 — Governance**) and re-verified annually.

**At application**

1. A **written, board-approved child protection and safeguarding policy**, dated within the last **24 months**, covering children and vulnerable adults.
2. A named **Designated Safeguarding Officer** with contact details, at a level senior enough to act, and a named deputy.
3. A **code of conduct** signed by every staff member and volunteer (Section 2).
4. A **POSH policy** and a constituted **Internal Committee** where the partner has **10 or more employees**, with at least half the members women and one external member from an NGO or a person familiar with issues of sexual harassment.
5. A **reporting and response procedure** with the timelines in Sections 4 and 5.
6. Disclosure of all safeguarding incidents, allegations and investigations in the last **five years** (**Partner Due Diligence Policy, Section 6 — Disclosures**).
7. A **safeguarding budget line** covering training, background checks and reporting materials, typically **0.5–1% of project cost**.

**Ongoing compliance**

- Annual **safeguarding self-assessment** submitted with the annual report.
- **Policy review** at least every **24 months**, with the board approval date recorded.
- **Annual safeguarding declaration** signed by the partner's Chief Executive confirming that all staff are trained, screened and under signed codes of conduct, and listing any incidents in the period.
- **POSH annual report** filed with the District Officer by **31 January** each year, with a copy to us.
- **Safeguarding spot-check** by our programme manager during at least **one field visit per year**, covering the training register, signed codes, screening records and the incident log.
- Failure to maintain compliance suspends disbursement under **Financial Policy, Section 6 — Disbursement** until remedied, with a remediation window of **30 days**.

## 2. Standards of Behaviour and Code of Conduct

Every adult working on a funded project signs a code of conduct before any contact with children. The code is not advisory.

**Prohibited absolutely**

- Any sexual activity, contact, language or suggestive behaviour with a person under 18, regardless of local age of consent or claimed consent.
- Any physical or corporal punishment, mental harassment, humiliation, ridicule or discriminatory treatment (RTE Act, Section 17).
- Exchanging money, employment, goods, services or preferential treatment for sexual favours or any other exploitative purpose.
- Being alone with a child in a closed, private or unobserved setting. Where one-to-one contact is necessary, such as individual reading assessment, it takes place in an **open or visible space** with another adult in sight.
- Transporting a child alone in a private vehicle.
- Personal contact outside project activity: private messaging, personal social media contact, home visits without a second adult, or sharing personal phone numbers.
- Photographing or recording a child without written guardian consent and the head teacher's permission; photographing a child undressed or in a demeaning position under any circumstance.
- Possessing, producing or distributing child sexual abuse material.
- Alcohol or drugs during project activity, or attending a project site under the influence.
- Working with children while carrying an undisclosed conviction or pending charge for an offence against children.

**Required**: two-adult presence at all group activity with children; age-appropriate, non-discriminatory behaviour equally respectful to girls and boys, children with disabilities and children of all social backgrounds; visible identification on school premises; and immediate reporting of any concern, including about a colleague or a government employee.

Breach of the code is a disciplinary matter. Substantiated breaches of the prohibited list result in **immediate termination** and, where the conduct is an offence, referral to the police.

## 3. Screening and Training

**Screening — before appointment, without exception**

- Verified **photo identity and address** proof, and verification of qualification documents.
- **Police verification** (character or antecedent verification from the local police or state portal) for every child-facing role, obtained before deployment or, where the certificate is delayed, within **60 days** of joining under supervised working.
- **Self-declaration** of no conviction, pending prosecution or prior dismissal for misconduct involving children.
- **Two reference checks**, one from the most recent employer, recorded, with a specific safeguarding question asked.
- Screening records retained for employment plus **three years**.

The same screening applies to volunteers, interns, consultants, enumerators and drivers at project sites.

**Training**

- **Induction safeguarding training**, minimum **3 hours**, before any contact with children, for every staff member and volunteer.
- **Annual refresher**, minimum **3 hours**, for all child-facing staff.
- **Designated Safeguarding Officer training**, minimum **one full day per year**, covering POCSO reporting duties, evidence handling and survivor-centred response.
- **POSH orientation** for all employees annually, with a dedicated session for Internal Committee members.
- A **training register** with dates, names, content and trainer, available for inspection.
- Children and SMC members must be told, in the local language, how to raise a concern and to whom: at least **one child-facing awareness session per school per year**.

## 4. Reporting

**Routes**

- Any concern is reported first to the partner's **Designated Safeguarding Officer**.
- Concerns involving the Designated Safeguarding Officer, the Chief Executive or a board member are reported directly to our **Designated Safeguarding Lead** on the dedicated safeguarding email address and telephone line published in the grant agreement.
- Children, parents and community members may report through the SMC, the head teacher, the display poster at the school, or the toll-free child helpline **1098**.

**Duties**

- Reporting is **mandatory for every person**, not discretionary. Under **POCSO Section 19**, anyone who apprehends that a sexual offence against a child is likely, or knows one has been committed, must report it to the Special Juvenile Police Unit or the local police. Failure to report is itself an offence under **POCSO Section 21**.
- The reporter need not investigate, verify or judge the credibility of the concern first.
- Reports may be anonymous and are protected under the partner's whistleblower policy. Retaliation against a reporter is a disciplinary offence.
- **Confidentiality**: need-to-know basis only. The child's identity is never disclosed publicly (POCSO Section 23). Records are held under the access controls in **Data Protection Policy, Section 4**.

## 5. Incident Reporting: Timelines and Response

| Step | Timeline |
|---|---|
| Concern raised to the partner's Designated Safeguarding Officer | **Immediately**, and in all cases within **24 hours** of becoming aware |
| Report to the police or Special Juvenile Police Unit where a POCSO offence is suspected | **Immediately**, and within **24 hours** |
| Notification to our Designated Safeguarding Lead, in writing | Within **24 hours** of the partner becoming aware |
| Immediate protective action (suspension of the accused from child contact, ensuring the child's safety, medical care if needed) | Within **24 hours** |
| Interim written update to us | Within **72 hours** |
| Investigation plan agreed | Within **7 days** |
| Investigation completed and written report submitted | Within **30 days** (extendable once, to **45 days**, with written reasons) |
| Notification to our Board Chair for any incident graded serious | Within **7 days** |
| POSH complaint: filing window | Within **90 days** of the incident (extendable by a further 90 days by the Internal Committee) |
| POSH inquiry completed by the Internal Committee | Within **90 days** of the complaint |
| POSH inquiry report submitted to the employer | Within **10 days** of completing the inquiry |
| Action on POSH recommendations | Within **60 days** of receiving the report |
| Case closure note and lessons-learned record | Within **14 days** of investigation close |

**Further rules**

- The accused is suspended from all child contact from the moment of report until the investigation concludes. Suspension is a protective measure, not a finding.
- We may suspend disbursement, commission an independent investigation, or terminate the grant at any point. Concealment or delayed reporting is a **material breach** of the grant agreement and grounds for recovery of funds.
- The partner may not settle, compromise or "resolve locally" any matter disclosing a criminal offence.
- Every incident, substantiated or not, is recorded in the partner's **incident log** and reported in the annual safeguarding declaration.
- Aggregate, anonymised safeguarding data is reported to our Board **twice a year**.
- Incident records are retained **seven years** after closure, or until the youngest child concerned reaches 25, whichever is later.
