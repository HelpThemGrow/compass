# Government Engagement Protocol

**Document ID:** VIB-FW-06
**Version:** 1.0
**Owner:** Head of Government Partnerships
**Approved by:** Grants Committee
**Review cycle:** Annual

---

## 0. Principle

We fund work inside the public education system (**Strategy Note, Section 1.2 — Public System Focus**). Government is not an audience for our projects; it is the delivery system. Every grant must therefore show who in government has authorised the work, who carries it, and what remains with government at the end.

Non-negotiable conduct rules, applying to us and to every partner:

- No cash, honorarium, gift, hospitality above a nominal working meal, or personal benefit to any serving government official. Reimbursement of actual travel and per-diem at prevailing government rates is permitted and must be shown in the budget under **Financial Policy, Section 2 — Budget Format**.
- No project activity in a school without written permission from the competent authority.
- No public statement, press release or social media post naming a government department without prior written clearance.
- No participation in, or funding of, any political or party activity.

## 1. Stakeholder Mapping

Every proposal must include a stakeholder map covering four tiers, naming the **office and designation** (never a named individual, whose posting changes) and the specific role each plays.

**State tier** — Principal Secretary / Secretary, School Education; State Project Director, Samagra Shiksha; Director, SCERT (curriculum, assessment, teacher-education alignment); State FLN / NIPUN Bharat Mission Director; Directorate of School Education and the State Assessment Board where assessments are involved.

**District tier** — District Collector / District Magistrate where district-wide convergence is needed; District Education Officer (DEO) and District Project Coordinator, Samagra Shiksha; Principal, DIET, the academic anchor for teacher development.

**Block tier** — Block Education Officer (BEO); Block Resource Centre Coordinator (BRC) and Block Resource Persons.

**Cluster and school tier** — Cluster Resource Centre Coordinator (CRC), the key academic supervisor for FLN work; head teacher and teachers; School Management Committee (SMC).

For each stakeholder, state what the project needs from them, what it offers, how often they will be engaged, and who owns the relationship. Minimum cadence for Tier 3 and Tier 4 grants: **one state review every six months, one district review every quarter and one block review every month**.

The map must also state the **entry point** — the office through which access is first obtained — and the **escalation route** if access stalls.

## 2. Permissions

No child-facing activity may begin without written permission. Verbal assurance is not permission.

**Required instruments**

| Scope of work | Instrument required | Issuing authority | Typical lead time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single block, non-curricular support | Written permission letter / NOC | Block Education Officer | 4–6 weeks |
| District-wide, in-school delivery | District NOC or order | District Education Officer / District Collector | 6–10 weeks |
| Multi-district or state programme | **MoU with the State Education Department or Samagra Shiksha** | Principal Secretary or State Project Director | 3–6 months |
| Curriculum, assessment or teacher-education content | Academic approval | SCERT | 8–12 weeks |
| Placement of technical staff inside a government office | MoU with a specific deputation clause | State department | 3–6 months |
| Data access (UDISE+, state MIS, child records) | Written data-sharing agreement | Data-owning department | 6–12 weeks |

**Rules**

- State the **current permission status**: obtained (with date and reference), applied for (with date), or not yet initiated. Attach copies as annexures.
- Where permission is not yet obtained, the work plan must allow at least **8 weeks** for it (**Programme Design Standards, Section 3.4 — Sequencing**) and it must appear on the risk register (**Risk Management SOP, Section 3 — Contextual Risk**).
- An MoU must specify scope, geography, duration, obligations of each party, government in-kind contributions, data ownership and sharing, safeguarding obligations, intellectual property, branding, the exit date, and a termination clause with notice of not less than **30 days**.
- MoU validity should match the grant period, with a review point at least **90 days** before expiry.
- Permissions must be **re-validated within 30 days** of a new officer taking charge of the signing authority.
- A separate data-sharing agreement is required before any child-level data is received from government, on terms satisfying **Data Protection Policy, Section 4 — Children's Data**.
- Where the intervention touches child safety, share the partner's safeguarding policy and staff police-verification status with the authority at application (**Safeguarding Policy, Section 3**).

## 3. Ways of Working and Convergence

Convergence means using the government's own structures, calendar, materials and people, rather than building a parallel apparatus.

- **Use the government cadre.** Route academic support through CRC and BRC coordinators and DIET faculty. By the final year of a Stage 3 or higher project (**Strategy Note, Section 4**), at least **50% of contact hours** should be delivered by government personnel.
- **Use the government calendar.** Train on days already designated for teacher development; no activity during examination weeks; plan against no more than **200 usable instructional days** per academic year.
- **Use government materials.** Align to the state textbook, the NIPUN Bharat competency framework and the state FLN handbook. Parallel curricula require SCERT approval.
- **Sit in existing forums.** Attend and report into the monthly BRC meeting, the quarterly district review and the state FLN review rather than convening project-only meetings.
- **Align to the AWP&B.** Identify the Samagra Shiksha Annual Work Plan and Budget line the project supports, and submit inputs to the state planning cycle, which typically closes between **December and February**.
- **Share data both ways.** Share project data with the block and district in the government's own format at least **quarterly**, with MIS fields mapped to UDISE+ codes.
- **Co-brand or de-brand.** School materials carry the government's identity first; partner branding on classroom materials requires written approval.

**Government contribution.** Every proposal must state what government contributes — school time and space, teacher time, textbooks, transport, officer time, and cash where applicable — valued in the budget as in-kind co-funding. For Stage 4 projects a **government cost-share of at least 25%** of total project cost is expected.

## 4. Institutionalisation

Institutionalisation is the point of the work. A model that only runs while we fund it has not scaled.

**Definition.** A model is institutionalised when the government owns the design, funds the recurring cost, and delivers it through its own people, with the partner in an advisory role or absent.

**Markers, in ascending order of strength**

1. The model is referenced in a district or state review agenda.
2. Government personnel are trained as trainers and deliver at least **50%** of training days.
3. The model's materials are adopted by SCERT or printed by the state.
4. The model is written into the **Samagra Shiksha AWP&B** with a budget line.
5. A government order (GO) or circular directs implementation.
6. The state funds at least **75%** of recurring cost and the partner's role is technical assistance only.

**Requirements on projects**

- Every grant above **INR 50 lakh** must name its institutionalisation target at proposal stage — which marker, by when — and report against it annually.
- A **transition plan** is due no later than the **mid-point of the final grant year**, naming the government owner for each function, the recurring cost and its source.
- **Cost of continuation** must be calculated and shared with government: the recurring per-child cost the state would carry, benchmarked against **Financial Policy, Section 3 — Value for Money**.
- **Capability transfer** must be evidenced: government trainers certified, materials handed over, systems documented in the local language, and SOPs deposited with the department.
- Intellectual property in materials developed with grant funds is licensed to government for non-commercial use at no cost, stated in the MoU.

---

## 5. Exit

Every grant ends. Exit is planned at entry, not negotiated at the end.

- The proposal must state the intended end state: institutionalisation, handover to another funder, government adoption, or closure.
- A written **exit plan** is required at least **6 months** before the end of the final grant year, and is a condition of the final tranche under **Financial Policy, Section 6 — Disbursement**.
- **Notice to government**: department, district and block informed in writing at least **90 days** before withdrawal.
- **Handover pack**, at least **30 days** before exit: training materials and master files, trained-personnel register, the dataset with child identifiers removed unless a lawful data-sharing agreement permits otherwise (**Data Protection Policy, Section 4**), SOPs, the asset register with transfer documentation, and the final evaluation report.
- **Assets** bought with grant funds transfer to the school, the department or another approved non-profit, with signed acknowledgement. Disposal or retention requires our written approval.
- **Staff** must be given notice consistent with their contracts; salaries may not be left unfunded at close.
- **Post-exit review**: for grants above **INR 50 lakh**, a light-touch review **12 months** after exit records what survived, feeding **MEL Framework, Section 5 — Learning and Adaptation**.

**Total funding limit.** Continuous funding of a single partner for a single model is capped at **five years** (**Strategy Note, Section 4**). Beyond that, either institutionalisation is demonstrated or the grant closes.
