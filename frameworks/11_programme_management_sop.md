# Programme Management Standard Operating Procedure

**Document ID:** VIB-FW-11
**Version:** 1.0
**Owner:** Director of Programmes
**Approved by:** Grants Committee
**Review cycle:** Annual

---

## 1. Purpose and Application

This SOP defines how a funded project is planned, staffed and run. It applies to every partner from the date of grant signing. Proposals are appraised against Sections 2 to 4: an implementation plan that cannot be staffed, or a work plan that ignores the school calendar, is a design fault, not a detail.

Grant management on our side is held by a **programme manager**, who carries no more than **8 active grants** at any time, or **5** where any grant is Tier 4 (above INR 2 crore).

---

## 2. Work Planning

### 2.1 The Work Plan

A month-by-month work plan is a mandatory annexure to every proposal and every renewal.

Required content:

- **Monthly grid** covering the full grant period, mapped to the Indian academic year (typically April/June to March), not to a generic 12-month cycle.
- Every activity from **Programme Design Standards, Section 3.3 — Activity Specification** appears with a start month, an end month and a responsible role.
- **Milestones**: at least **one milestone per quarter**, each defined so that it can be verified as done or not done.
- **Dependencies** marked: which activities cannot start until another completes.
- **Non-working periods** blocked out: summer vacation, examination weeks, festival closures and known election periods. Plan on no more than **200 usable instructional days** per academic year and state the figure used.
- **Lead times** allowed: at least **8 weeks** for government permissions (**Government Engagement Protocol, Section 2**), **6 weeks** for recruitment, **4 weeks** for material printing, **60 days** for baseline completion from grant start (**MEL Framework, Section 3.3**).
- **Year-one detail** at monthly resolution; later years may be at quarterly resolution but must be revised to monthly detail **60 days before** each year begins.

### 2.2 Inception and Revision

- An **inception period of up to 60 days** from grant signing covers recruitment, permissions, procurement, baseline and system set-up. Child-facing delivery may begin only once the baseline is under way.
- An **inception report** — revised work plan, staffing confirmed, permissions obtained, baseline design — is submitted within **60 days** of signing.
- The work plan is reviewed at every quarterly joint review and formally revised **once a year**.
- Any change altering activities, geography or targets by more than **10%** requires prior written approval (**MEL Framework, Section 5 — Learning and Adaptation**), with budget consequences under **Financial Policy, Section 7.2 — Variance**.
- Activity completion below **80%** of the plan at the half-year point requires a written catch-up plan within **21 days**.

## 3. Staffing

### 3.1 Structure and Roles

The proposal must include an organogram and a role table listing, for every post: title, number of posts, reporting line, percentage of time charged to the grant, qualification required, and whether the post is filled or to be recruited.

Minimum structure for a district-scale project:

- **Project Lead** — one per project, minimum 50% time charged, accountable for delivery, government relations and reporting.
- **Programme / Academic Lead** — designs training content, quality-assures classroom practice.
- **Block or Cluster Coordinators** — field supervision (ratios in Section 4.3).
- **Field Facilitators / Teacher Mentors** — direct delivery or mentoring.
- **MEL Officer** — mandatory for every grant above **INR 50 lakh**; at least 50% time.
- **Finance and Compliance Officer** — mandatory for every grant above **INR 50 lakh**.
- **Designated Safeguarding Officer** and **Data Custodian** — named roles, which may be held alongside another post (**Safeguarding Policy, Section 1**; **Data Protection Policy, Section 2**).

### 3.2 Recruitment and Retention

- All child-facing staff must complete screening before deployment: police verification, two reference checks, self-declaration (**Safeguarding Policy, Section 3 — Screening and Training**).
- Induction — minimum **5 days**, including **3 hours** of safeguarding and **2 hours** of data protection — before any school contact.
- Refresher training for field staff: minimum **6 days per year**.
- **Vacancy rule**: any position vacant for more than **60 days** must be reported to the programme manager with a recruitment plan; salary savings may not be redeployed without approval under the variance rule.
- **Attrition**: plan on **20–30% annual attrition** in rural field roles. Budgets must include recruitment and re-induction costs.
- **Local recruitment** is expected: at least **70%** of field staff should be from the project district or an adjoining district, and must be fluent in the language of instruction.
- All staff on written contracts, paid by bank transfer, with PF and ESI as applicable (**Compliance Policy, Section 2.5**).

## 4. Operating Systems

### 4.1 Management Information System (MIS)

- Every project above **INR 10 lakh** must run a digital MIS. Paper-only records are acceptable only for Tier 1 grants.
- The MIS must hold, at minimum: school master data keyed to **UDISE+ codes**, child enrolment and attendance, session delivery records, teacher training and observation records, assessment results, and staff field-visit logs.
- **Data entry within 7 days** of the field activity. Records older than 30 days entered retrospectively are flagged.
- Child records use a **pseudonymous child code**; the linking key is held separately under **Data Protection Policy, Section 4.3**.
- **Data quality checks**: automated range and completeness validation on entry; a manual **5% record spot-check monthly** by the field supervisor; a **data quality audit twice a year** (**MEL Framework, Section 3.3**).
- MIS fields must be mappable to government formats so that data can be shared with the block and district quarterly (**Government Engagement Protocol, Section 3**).
- Access is role-based; access lists are reviewed **quarterly**.

### 4.2 Reporting Cadence

| Report | From → To | Due |
|---|---|---|
| Field visit report | Field staff → Block Coordinator | Within **3 working days** of the visit |
| Monthly MIS and progress summary | Partner → our programme manager | By the **10th** of the following month |
| Quarterly narrative and utilisation report | Partner → us | Within **30 days** of quarter end |
| Quarterly joint review meeting, minuted | Partner and our programme manager | Within **45 days** of quarter end |
| Updated risk register | Partner → us | With the quarterly report |
| Annual report (narrative, results, financial) | Partner → us | Within **60 days** of year end |
| Annual utilisation certificate | Partner → us | Within **90 days** of financial year end |
| End-of-grant report | Partner → us | Within **90 days** of grant close |
| Field visit by our programme manager | Us | Tier 1: 1 per grant; Tier 2: 2 per year; Tier 3 and 4: 4 per year |

Late reporting beyond **15 days** past the due date is recorded; two late reports in a year withhold the next tranche (**Financial Policy, Section 6 — Disbursement**).

### 4.3 Field Supervision Ratios

These ratios are the appraisal benchmark. A proposal outside them must justify the difference.

| Role | Ratio | Minimum contact |
|---|---|---|
| Field Facilitator (direct delivery) | 1 : 5 schools, or 1 : 150 children | Daily presence in assigned schools |
| Teacher Mentor (in-service support) | 1 : 20 teachers | **1 on-site visit per teacher per month** |
| Cluster Coordinator | 1 : 20 schools | **2 school visits per school per month** |
| Block Coordinator | 1 : 6 field staff, or 1 : 100 schools | Weekly team meeting; 8 school visits per month |
| Project Lead | 1 : 6 block or cluster coordinators | Monthly review; 4 school visits per month |
| MEL Officer | 1 : 200 schools | Quarterly data audit |

Additional standards:

- Every school receives at least **one supervisory visit per month** during active delivery.
- Every training event is observed by a supervisor for at least **50%** of its duration.
- Classroom observation: each participating teacher observed at least **once per term** using a structured schedule (**MEL Framework, Section 3.1**).
- Supervisor travel days must be budgeted; a field role budgeted with fewer than **15 field days per month** will be queried.

### 4.4 Escalation

| Trigger | Escalate to | Within |
|---|---|---|
| Safeguarding concern of any kind | Designated Safeguarding Officer, then our Designated Safeguarding Lead | **24 hours** (**Safeguarding Policy, Section 5**) |
| Suspected fraud or misappropriation | Partner Chief Executive and our Director of Finance | **24 hours** (**Financial Policy, Section 8**) |
| Data breach | Partner Data Custodian, then our Data Protection Officer | **6 hours** internally, **24 hours** to us |
| Materialised risk scored 15–25 (critical) | Our Director of Programmes | **7 days** (**Risk Management SOP, Section 4**) |
| Field issue unresolved at school level | Cluster Coordinator | **7 days** |
| Issue unresolved at cluster level | Block Coordinator and Project Lead | **14 days** |
| Issue unresolved at project level | Our programme manager | **21 days** |
| Government permission withdrawn or access blocked | Our programme manager and Head of Government Partnerships | **48 hours** |
| Statutory notice or penalty received | Our Head of Compliance | **7 days** (**Compliance Policy, Section 3**) |
| Departure of the Project Lead or a named key person | Our programme manager | **7 days**, with a replacement plan within **30 days** |

Escalation is a duty, not a discretion. Failure to escalate within these windows is recorded on the partner file and weighed at renewal under **Renewal Policy, Section 2.4 — Candour**.
