# Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning (MEL) Framework

**Document ID:** VIB-FW-03
**Version:** 1.0
**Owner:** Head of Monitoring and Evaluation
**Approved by:** Grants Committee
**Review cycle:** Annual

---

## 1. Purpose and Application

This framework sets the measurement standard for every funded project: what counts as an indicator, how data is collected, what must be reported, when independent evaluation is compulsory, and what may be claimed.

MEL costs are a **direct programme cost**, not overhead, and should fall between **3% and 7% of total project cost**. Above 7% requires justification in the budget narrative.

---

## 2. Indicator Standards

Every project submits a results framework with a minimum of **8 and a maximum of 20 indicators**.

### 2.1 Indicator Definition

Each indicator carries all nine fields; one missing field means the indicator is not accepted:

1. Indicator statement (what changes, in whom).
2. Level: input, output, outcome or impact.
3. Unit of measure and, where relevant, the denominator.
4. Baseline value and date.
5. Target value, with milestones for each year of the grant.
6. Data source and instrument.
7. Frequency of collection.
8. Person responsible for collection and verification.
9. Disaggregation: at minimum by sex and grade; by social category and disability status where targeted.

Indicators must be **SMART**. "Improved learning" is not an indicator. "Percentage of Grade 3 children reading a Grade 2 level text with comprehension, ASER-equivalent tool, rising from 24% to 45% by March 2027" is.

### 2.2 Mandatory Indicators

Every project must include, whatever else it measures: at least **one child learning outcome indicator** assessed directly on children; **attendance of the target cohort**, sampled at least monthly; **activity completion rate** against the work plan; and **cost per child reached**, updated annually.

### 2.3 Output vs Outcome

- An **output** is what the project produces and controls: 240 teachers trained, 96 sessions delivered, 12,000 workbooks distributed, 85% attendance at training.
- An **outcome** is what changes in someone else as a result: teachers using level-based grouping in 70% of observed lessons, children moving from letter level to word level, head teachers running monthly academic reviews.
- **Impact** is the longer-term system or population change: district-wide Grade 3 reading proficiency, state adoption of the assessment tool.

Rules:

- The framework must contain at least **three outcome indicators**. A framework of outputs only is returned.
- Numbers trained, reached or distributed may never be reported as outcomes.
- Every outcome indicator must have a named measurement instrument, not an inference from output data.
- Where an outcome cannot be measured within the grant period, name an intermediate outcome that can, and state the lag.

## 3. Data Collection and Instruments

### 3.1 Instruments

Each instrument must be named, described and annexed in draft. Acceptable instruments:

- **Learning assessment**: ASER-style oral reading and arithmetic tools, state FLN tools, NIPUN Bharat competency checklists, or a project tool with stated construction and, for Tier 3 and above, evidence of reliability.
- **Classroom observation**: structured schedule with a defined scoring scale, used by trained observers.
- **Attendance registers**: school register plus independent spot-count.
- **Teacher and SMC interviews or surveys**: semi-structured, schedule annexed.
- **Administrative data**: UDISE+, state MIS, school records.

Enumerators must be trained for at least **two days** before each major round and must not assess children they have taught. At least **30% of the sample** must be assessed by someone independent of the delivery team.

### 3.2 Sampling

- **Census** for output and attendance data.
- **Sample** for learning assessment: minimum **30 children per school stratum**, and no fewer than **384 children** in total where a project-level estimate at 95% confidence and ±5% precision is claimed.
- Schools sampled: the greater of **10% of project schools or 30 schools**.
- State the sampling method (simple random, stratified, systematic). Convenience sampling is not accepted for outcome claims.
- Track the **same cohort** from baseline to endline. Attrition above **20%** must be reported and analysed; above **30%** the endline is indicative only.

### 3.3 Frequency

| Data type | Minimum frequency |
|---|---|
| Attendance and activity completion | Monthly |
| Classroom observation | Once per term per teacher (3 per year) |
| Learning assessment | Baseline within 60 days of grant start; midline at 12 months; endline within 60 days of grant end |
| Teacher and SMC feedback | Once per term |
| Data quality audit | Twice per year |
| Field spot-check by partner supervisor | 5% of records per month |

Baseline data must be shared with us within **30 days** of collection. Failure to complete a baseline within the first 90 days of a grant is a trigger for withholding the second tranche under **Financial Policy, Section 6 — Disbursement**.

## 4. Outcome Reporting

Reports must present results, not activity narratives.

- Report **actual against target** for every indicator, including those missed. Selective reporting is a candour failure under **Renewal Policy, Section 2.4 — Candour**.
- Report the **denominator** with every percentage. "68% improvement" without a base is not accepted.
- Report **learning gains in comparable units**: percentage-point change at a defined competency level, standard-deviation change, or grade-level equivalent.
- Report results **disaggregated** by sex and grade as a minimum.
- State the **assessment date, tool, sample size and attrition** for every learning figure.
- Distinguish children **enrolled**, **reached** (attended at least 60% of sessions) and **assessed**. Headline reach uses children reached.
- Report negative or null results in the same detail as positive results.

Cadence: quarterly progress report within **30 days** of quarter end; annual report within **60 days** of year end; end-of-grant report within **90 days** of close. These sit alongside **Financial Policy, Section 7 — Utilisation Reporting**.

## 5. Learning and Adaptation

Measurement exists to change decisions. Every project must show a working feedback loop.

- A **quarterly review meeting** between the partner's programme lead and our programme manager, minuted, with at least one decision recorded.
- An **annual learning review** with the field team, documented in a note of no more than four pages naming what changed and why.
- Any mid-course design change altering activities, geography or targets by more than **10%** must be approved in writing before implementation. Budget consequences follow **Financial Policy, Section 7.2 — Variance**.
- Partners must record **what did not work**. Renewals reporting no failures are treated as incomplete (**Renewal Policy, Section 2.4 — Candour**).
- We publish anonymised portfolio learning at least **once a year**; partners agree to this at signing.

## 6. Verification

Verification is proportionate to grant size. Grant tiers are the same tiers used in **Funding Policy, Section 2** and **Partner Due Diligence Policy, Section 3**.

| Grant size | Verification requirement |
|---|---|
| Under INR 10 lakh | Partner self-report; one field visit by our programme manager during the grant |
| INR 10–50 lakh | Partner self-report; independent verification of a **5% sample** of learning data; two field visits per year |
| INR 50 lakh – 2 crore | **Independent external evaluation is mandatory**; midline and endline; evaluator appointed by us or jointly, and not previously contracted by the partner for this project |
| Above INR 2 crore | Independent external evaluation with a **comparison group** (RCT, quasi-experimental or matched design); baseline, midline and endline; evaluation protocol approved before baseline |

Further rules:

- **Independent evaluation is mandatory for every grant above INR 50 lakh.** Evaluation cost is budgeted separately and typically falls between **2% and 5% of grant value**.
- The evaluator reports to us, not the partner. The partner may respond in writing; the response is annexed, not merged.
- We may conduct field verification with **48 hours' notice**, and a data quality audit once per year.
- Discrepancy above **10%** between reported and verified figures triggers a formal review and may suspend disbursement.

## 7. Attribution

Claims must match the evidence design.

- Without a comparison group, the correct language is **contribution**, not attribution. Say "children in project schools improved by X percentage points" and state what else was happening in those schools.
- **Attribution requires a counterfactual**: a comparison group, difference-in-differences, regression discontinuity or randomised assignment. Normally only projects above INR 50 lakh, which carry mandatory independent evaluation, will have this.
- Where government is running a parallel FLN drive in the same schools, say so and do not claim the full gain.
- Pre-post change alone is reported as **observed change**, never as impact.
- Report effect sizes with sample size and, where computed, the confidence interval. An effect below **0.10 SD** is reported as not distinguishable from no effect unless the design supports a tighter claim.
- Reach may not be double-counted across years: a child served in two years counts once in cumulative reach, and the report must state whether figures are annual or cumulative.
