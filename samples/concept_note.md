# Sunehri Kitaab: School Libraries That Actually Get Used

This concept note outlines an early-stage proposal from Saathi Gyan Kendra for a one-year reading and library activation pilot in Bhairavganj block, Rampur district.

**Contact:** Ms Meenakshi Rathore, Programme Lead — meenakshi.rathore@saathigyan.example.org — +91 90000 66341
**Indicative funding sought:** INR 18,00,000
**Duration:** 12 months
**Children reached:** 3,200

## Summary

Most government primary schools in Bhairavganj block have books. Very few have reading. Book sets arrive under Samagra Shiksha library grants, are entered in a stock register, and are then kept in a locked almirah because nobody wants to answer for a damaged or missing book. *Sunehri Kitaab* proposes a small, deliberately cheap pilot to test whether a light-touch package — a levelled and colour-coded book set, a weekly timetabled library period, a simple issue-return system a child can run, and a monthly reading circle for teachers — moves books out of the almirah and into children's hands.

This is a pilot, not a programme. The purpose of the year is to find out whether the model is worth designing properly, and to produce a costed note the block education office could act on.

## Problem

Bhairavganj block has 74 government primary schools. In an informal walkthrough of 22 of them in May and June 2026, Saathi Gyan Kendra found that 19 had a book collection of some kind, 4 had a timetabled library period, and 2 could show any record of a book having been issued to a child in the previous three months. Head teachers gave a consistent explanation: books are an audited asset, there is no protocol for loss, and the safest thing to do with them is to keep them locked.

Meanwhile, children who can decode still do not read. Grade 4 and 5 teachers describe children who read a text aloud but cannot say what it was about — the gap between decoding and comprehension that a reading habit, more than further instruction, tends to close.

This is observation, not evidence. We have no formal baseline; the figures above come from an unstructured visit rather than a sampled survey. Establishing a baseline is the first activity of the pilot.

## Proposed Approach

**Levelled and colour-coded book sets.** Each school receives 240 titles in four colour bands by reading difficulty, so a child can choose at their own level without a teacher mediating every choice.

**A timetabled weekly library period.** Forty minutes, once a week, for Grades 2 to 5. Protected time is the biggest predictor of whether a library is used.

**A child-run issue-return system.** Two library monitors per class, a card-and-pocket system, and a written loss norm agreed with the head teacher so a lost book is not a personal liability. Removing the fear of loss is the point of this component.

**Monthly teacher reading circles.** One hour a month at the Cluster Resource Centre on read-aloud technique and comprehension talk, with the Cluster Resource Coordinator invited from the start.

**Termly reading festival.** A morning of read-alouds and book talks with parents and the School Management Committee.

## Expected Outcomes

By the end of twelve months, in the pilot schools:

- A timetabled library period running in at least 80 per cent of schools.
- At least 12 books issued per child over the year, tracked from the issue register.
- Coordinators observing more read-aloud practice in class.
- An early signal on comprehension from a small Grade 4 and 5 assessment at the start and end of the year.

The comprehension measurement will be small, internal and single-group; it will tell us whether the model is worth developing, not whether it works. A full proposal would need an independent assessment with comparison schools, costed in.

## Scale and Geography

Bhairavganj block, Rampur district. Forty of the block's 74 government primary schools, selected to span school size and distance from the block headquarters. Grades 2 to 5. Approximately 3,200 children and 120 teachers.

Bhairavganj holds the block's largest number of unused Samagra Shiksha library grants, and the Block Education Officer has verbally encouraged a pilot. If the model holds, the next step is the remaining 34 schools — a decision for a later proposal.

## About the Organisation

Saathi Gyan Kendra is a registered society working on children's reading in Rampur district since 2016, with an annual budget of approximately INR 62 lakh and a team of nine. We have run book-based reading work in 26 schools of Kesarganj block since 2022 for a domestic donor.

We hold Societies registration, 12A and 80G, and are CSR-1 filed. We do not hold FCRA registration and cannot accept foreign contributions. Our child protection policy was adopted in 2023 and all field staff undergo police verification. Registration numbers, audited accounts and the safeguarding policy are available on request. We have not previously managed a grant of this size and would welcome a conversation about reporting expectations first.
