## Renewal Summary

This is a **renewal** request for the continuation of *Padho Rampur*, a Grade 3 to 5 reading and comprehension programme that Ujjwal Bal Vikas Sansthan (UBVS) has run in 96 government primary schools of Kesarganj and Bhairavganj blocks, Rampur district, under grant GR-2024-0117. The continuation would extend the programme for a further 24 months, hold the same 96 schools, and shift the emphasis from direct delivery towards handover to the Cluster Resource Centre structure.

Over the completed grant period we reached 8,640 children and 288 teachers against a target of 8,400 children and 288 teachers. We met or exceeded eleven of our sixteen delivery targets and missed five, which are set out honestly in the variance section below rather than averaged away. Self-reported end-line assessment of 2,140 children shows the proportion reading a Grade 2 level text rising from 26.4 per cent at baseline (May 2024) to 47.8 per cent at end-line (March 2026).

We should be clear about the limits of that evidence. The assessment was conducted by our own field staff, there was no comparison group, and the design is a single-group before-and-after comparison. We believe the programme contributed materially to the change, but we cannot separate its effect from grade progression, other government inputs, or the recovery of attendance in the period. Independent verification is the principal weakness of this submission and we have proposed, in the next phase, to fund an external end-line — though we have not yet identified or costed a comparison block.

Financial utilisation stands at 95.25 per cent, with an unspent balance of INR 4,56,000 which we request permission to carry forward. The utilisation certificate for the full grant period, signed by M/s Bhatt & Menon, Chartered Accountants, is attached.

## Variance Analysis

**Teachers trained (271 against 288).** Seventeen teachers could not be trained because they were transferred out between the training calendar being fixed and the module being delivered, and their replacements arrived after the final cohort had run. We did not hold an additional cohort for seventeen people because the per-head cost would have been roughly three times the standard rate. In the next phase we will run a short mid-year induction cohort specifically for new arrivals.

**Coaching visits (4,102 against 4,608 — a shortfall of 11 per cent).** Three causes. First, 34 school days were lost across the two blocks to election duty, examination duty and a nine-day weather closure in July 2025. Second, one Field Coordinator position in Bhairavganj was vacant for four months following a resignation in September 2024; recruitment in that block was harder than we assumed. Third, we underestimated travel time in the eleven schools of the Sonpura cluster, where the monsoon route adds forty minutes each way. We have re-based the visit norm for that cluster in the next phase rather than continuing to plan a target we know we cannot meet.

**Cluster meetings (361 against 384).** Directly linked to the same vacancy and closure days above.

**SMC meetings supported (344 against 384).** In 40 instances the school did not convene a meeting in the quarter and we were not in a position to convene one ourselves. This is a real limitation of a model that depends on the school calling the meeting. We have reduced the next-phase target to a level we can actually influence and added a written prompt to head teachers a fortnight in advance.

**Community volunteer reading sessions (1,388 against 1,920 — a shortfall of 28 per cent, our largest miss).** The volunteer model did not work as designed. We recruited 96 volunteers but retention after six months was 44 per cent, largely because the sessions were unpaid and clashed with agricultural work. We are not proposing to continue this component in its present form.

## Learning and Adaptation

Four changes have already been made or are proposed for the next phase.

1. **Volunteer component discontinued.** Rather than repeat a component with 44 per cent retention, the next phase moves take-home reading support to the SMC and to a structured parent reading pledge, and reallocates the freed budget to cluster-level teacher work.
2. **Visit norms re-based by cluster.** A single district-wide visit norm produced a systematic shortfall in the two most dispersed clusters. Next-phase targets are set cluster by cluster.
3. **Assessment turnaround shortened.** In Year 1 results reached teachers roughly three weeks after assessment, by which point they were of little instructional use. From October 2025 we moved to a tablet-based entry with a 72-hour class sheet. Teacher use of the sheet, as observed by coordinators, rose noticeably after the change.
4. **Head teacher engagement front-loaded.** Schools where the head teacher attended the orientation before the first coaching visit showed better protection of the reading period. In the next phase the orientation is a precondition for starting coaching in that school.

**Reading levels.** UBVS field staff assessed a rolling sample of children using an ASER-style tool at six points.

**Comprehension.** The proportion of Grade 5 children answering at least three of four inferential comprehension questions correctly rose from 19.2 per cent to 38.4 per cent over the same period.

**Attendance.** Average daily attendance in Grades 3 to 5 in project schools rose from 71 per cent to 79 per cent, based on sampled school registers.

**How much of this can be attributed to the programme.** We want to be straightforward here. This is a single-group before-and-after comparison with no control or matched comparison schools. The assessment was designed, administered and scored by UBVS staff, who knew which children were in the programme; there was no blinding and no third-party verification of any round. A share of the improvement is likely to reflect ordinary grade progression and the district-wide attendance recovery after 2023. We have not attempted to estimate that share. Statements elsewhere in this document that the programme "improved" reading should be read as association, not established causation. We regard closing this gap as the main methodological task of the next phase.

## Proposed Next Phase

The next phase keeps the same 96 schools and the same grade range, and changes the delivery mix in three ways.

1. **From direct coaching to cluster-led practice.** Field Coordinator visits reduce from fortnightly to monthly by Month 12. The cluster teacher meeting becomes the main vehicle and is progressively co-facilitated, then led, by the Cluster Resource Coordinator.
2. **Volunteer component replaced.** The unpaid volunteer reading session is discontinued. Take-home reading moves to a structured parent pledge managed through the SMC, with a termly reading mela as the visible event.
3. **Independent end-line.** We have budgeted INR 6,00,000 for an external agency to conduct the Month 22 end-line. We should note plainly that we have not yet identified the agency, and we have not costed a matched comparison block; if the funder considers a comparison design essential, we would need to discuss additional budget.

The phase also adds a mid-year induction cohort for newly transferred teachers, and a costed handover note for the District Education Office in Month 20.

## Path to Exit or Institutionalisation

UBVS intends this to be the final grant-funded phase in these 96 schools.

- **Months 1–8.** Cluster Resource Coordinators observe and co-plan every cluster meeting; the teacher handbook is revised into a version that assumes no UBVS facilitator is present.
- **Months 9–16.** CRCs lead at least 30 per cent of cluster meetings; UBVS coaching visits step down from fortnightly to monthly; the reading corner maintenance protocol is transferred to head teachers with a one-page checklist.
- **Months 17–24.** CRCs lead at least 65 per cent of meetings; a costed handover note is presented to the District Education Officer and the Samagra Shiksha district unit in Month 20; final dissemination in Month 24.

At exit UBVS will hand over the handbook and levelled reader specifications with print-ready files, the assessment tool and scoring guide, the anonymised child-level dataset, and a per-block cost of running the model without UBVS. We have discussed the handover in principle with the Block Education Officer, Kesarganj; nothing has been committed in writing yet, and securing a written statement of intent from the District Education Office is a Month 6 milestone.
