# Buniyaad: Foundational Literacy and Numeracy at Scale in Rampur District

**Submitted by:** Prayaas Shiksha Foundation, Sundarpur, Rampur District
**Proposal type:** New project funding request
**Duration:** 36 months (1 April 2026 – 31 March 2029)
**Amount requested:** INR 3,00,00,000 (Rupees Three Crore only)
**Date of submission:** 12 August 2026

## Executive Summary

Prayaas Shiksha Foundation (PSF) requests INR 3,00,00,000 over 36 months to deliver *Buniyaad*, a foundational literacy and numeracy (FLN) programme in 180 government primary schools across Sundarpur and Kesarganj blocks of Rampur district. The project will directly reach 14,400 children in Grades 1 to 3 and 540 government teachers, working entirely inside the government school system in alignment with the NIPUN Bharat Mission.

ASER 2024 (rural, district supplementary tables, 28 January 2025) records that only 21.4 per cent of Grade 3 children in Rampur district can read a Grade 2 level text, against a state figure of 28.9 per cent; only 18.7 per cent can do two-digit subtraction with borrowing. UDISE+ 2023-24 shows 41 per cent of the district's primary schools are two-teacher schools where multi-grade teaching is the norm.

*Buniyaad* responds with a teacher-led classroom model: a levelled FLN instructional block delivered daily by the regular government teacher, fortnightly on-site coaching from a PSF Learning Facilitator, monthly cluster teacher learning circles anchored at the CRC, and a termly assessment cycle whose data flows back to the BRC and block education office. PSF deploys no parallel teaching staff; the intervention is designed to be absorbed by the Samagra Shiksha structure at exit.

Cost per child is INR 2,083 over three years (INR 694 per child per year), against a benchmark range of INR 900 to INR 1,400 per child per year for comparable in-school FLN remediation. Administrative overhead is 12.0 per cent of the total grant, below our 15 per cent ceiling.

By March 2029 we target 55 per cent of Grade 3 children reading a Grade 2 text (from 21.4 per cent) and 48 per cent competent in two-digit subtraction with borrowing (from 18.7 per cent), verified by an independent end-line evaluation with a matched comparison block.

## Context

Prayaas Shiksha Foundation (PSF) has operated in Rampur district since 2014, currently running an after-school reading programme in 60 government schools in Sundarpur block under a separate, smaller grant. This proposal is a new, larger initiative to bring a classroom-embedded FLN model to the full 180 schools of Sundarpur and Kesarganj blocks, working through the government system rather than alongside it - see Strategic Alignment below.

## Problem Statement

Rampur district has 412 government primary schools serving approximately 61,000 children in Grades 1 to 5. The learning gap is documented across three independent sources and our own baseline.

**ASER 2024 (rural), district supplementary tables, 28 January 2025.** Grade 3 reading at Grade 2 level: 21.4 per cent (state 28.9 per cent, national rural 23.4 per cent). Grade 3 two-digit subtraction with borrowing: 18.7 per cent. Grade 5 reading at Grade 2 level: 39.2 per cent, indicating the deficit accumulated in Grades 1 to 3 is never recovered.

**UDISE+ 2023-24, published 6 December 2024.** Rampur district: 41 per cent of government primary schools are two-teacher schools; average pupil-teacher ratio 33:1 with 180 schools above 35:1; Grade 1 to 5 retention 91.4 per cent, but average daily attendance in Grades 1 to 3 recorded at 68 per cent.

**NAS 2021 state report card (SCERT, Grade 3 language and mathematics).** Rampur district mean scale score in Grade 3 mathematics 279, against a state and national mean of 306; language 291 against a state mean of 314.

**Our own baseline, October 2025.** PSF assessed a random sample of 40 schools across the two proposed blocks using the ASER tool (n = 1,206 children, Grades 2 and 3). Findings: 22.1 per cent of Grade 3 children read a Grade 2 text; 19.4 per cent could do two-digit subtraction with borrowing; 34.8 per cent were at "letter" level or below. The sample was drawn with the written permission of the Block Education Officer, Sundarpur (letter no. BEO/SDP/2025/418 dated 2 September 2025).

Three drivers explain the gap, established through 62 classroom observations and 24 teacher interviews between August and November 2025:

1. **No differentiated instruction.** In 54 of 62 observed classrooms, teachers taught to the grade textbook page regardless of the spread of actual reading levels. Children below level disengaged within minutes.
2. **Teacher training is episodic and off-site.** Teachers reported an average of 2.3 days of FLN training in the preceding twelve months, delivered as a one-off residential module with no classroom follow-up.
3. **Assessment data does not reach the teacher.** Assessment is conducted for reporting upward. Only 4 of 24 teachers could say how many children in their own class were below grade level.

## Strategic Alignment

*Buniyaad* is designed to sit inside, not alongside, existing government architecture.

- **NIPUN Bharat Mission (2021):** learning outcome targets are mapped directly to the NIPUN *Lakshya Soochi* for Grades 1 to 3; the termly assessment instrument uses NIPUN competency codes.
- **Samagra Shiksha:** the programme uses the existing BRC/CRC academic support structure as its delivery spine rather than creating a parallel cadre. Cluster Resource Coordinators co-facilitate the monthly teacher learning circles from Year 2.
- **SCERT:** the teacher handbook and levelled reading card set will be submitted for academic review in Month 8, with the intent that a reviewed version enters the SCERT resource repository.
- **DIET:** the District Institute of Education and Training, Rampur, will co-deliver the annual five-day residential module and place two faculty on the project's academic advisory group.
- **NEP 2020, Chapter 2:** addresses the national priority that foundational literacy and numeracy is a prerequisite for all further schooling.

## Theory of Change

**Inputs.** INR 3,00,00,000 over 36 months; 12 Learning Facilitators, 3 Block Coordinators, 1 Project Manager, 1 Assessment and Data Officer; levelled card sets and numeracy kits for 180 schools; a teacher handbook mapped to NIPUN competencies; a tablet assessment application; MoUs with the District Education Office and both BEOs.

**Activities.** Daily 60-minute levelled FLN block taught by the regular government teacher; fortnightly in-classroom coaching by a Learning Facilitator; monthly three-hour cluster circle at the CRC; annual five-day residential module with DIET; termly child-level assessment; quarterly BEO data review; twice-yearly SMC meeting support.

**Outputs.** 540 teachers trained and coached; 180 schools running the daily FLN block on 80 per cent or more of school days; 14,400 children assessed three times a year; 36 cluster circles a month; four block data reviews a year.

**Outcomes.** (a) Teachers group children by reading level and teach to level, measured by observation rubric; (b) reading and numeracy levels rise against the NIPUN *Lakshya Soochi*; (c) the block education office uses termly learning data in its own reviews.

**Impact.** By 2029, children completing Grade 3 in the two blocks enter Grade 4 able to learn from the grade-level curriculum, and the district retains a costed, SCERT-reviewed model it can extend to Rampur's remaining six blocks.

### Assumptions

1. Teachers remain posted for at least two consecutive academic years; transfer churn stays under 20 per cent annually.
2. Schools function for at least 180 instructional days a year without prolonged closure.
3. The BEOs of Sundarpur and Kesarganj continue to permit and endorse in-school access as recorded in the MoU.
4. Average daily attendance in Grades 1 to 3 rises above the current 68 per cent through the SMC component; below 60 per cent, dosage becomes insufficient.
5. Teachers will reorganise the timetable to protect a 60-minute FLN block — validated with 24 teachers in design.
6. Samagra Shiksha textbook and TLM supply continues at present levels; *Buniyaad* materials supplement, not substitute.
7. No competing state-mandated programme occupies the same instructional slot.

## Project Design

**Geography.** Rampur district; Sundarpur block (98 schools) and Kesarganj block (82 schools); 180 government primary schools in total. Grades 1, 2 and 3. Estimated 14,400 children (average 80 per school across Grades 1 to 3) and 540 teachers.

**Component 1 — Daily levelled FLN block.** Delivered by the regular government teacher, 60 minutes daily, five days a week, across the full academic year. Children are grouped by assessed reading level, not by grade. PSF supplies levelled reading cards, a decodable reader set and a numeracy kit per classroom.

**Component 2 — In-classroom coaching.** Each Learning Facilitator covers 15 schools, visiting each fortnightly for a half-day on a fixed protocol: 30 minutes of observation against a five-point rubric, a 20-minute co-taught segment, a 20-minute feedback conversation. Twelve Learning Facilitators deliver approximately 360 school visits a month.

**Component 3 — Cluster teacher learning circles.** Monthly, three hours, at the Cluster Resource Centre; ten circles per block per month. Facilitated by the PSF Block Coordinator in Year 1, co-facilitated with the Cluster Resource Coordinator in Year 2, led by the CRC with PSF observing in Year 3.

**Component 4 — Residential module with DIET.** Five days, once a year, for all 540 teachers in cohorts of 60, co-delivered with DIET Rampur faculty.

**Component 5 — Assessment and data.** Three child-level assessments a year (July, December, March) on a tablet application. Results reach the teacher within 72 hours as a class-level "who is where" sheet, and the BEO as a block dashboard within two weeks.

**Component 6 — Community and SMC.** Two SMC meetings per school per year on attendance and reading at home, plus a Grade 3 "reading mela" each February.

## Monitoring and Evaluation

Monitoring is internal and continuous; evaluation is external and periodic.

**Internal.** Learning Facilitators record every school visit in the tablet application on the day of the visit. Block Coordinators independently re-verify a 10 per cent random sample of visit records each month by revisiting the school. The Assessment and Data Officer produces a monthly dashboard reviewed by the Project Manager and shared with the PSF board quarterly.

**External.** An independent evaluation agency, competitively selected in Month 2, will conduct a baseline (Month 3), mid-line (Month 20) and end-line (Month 34). The design is a matched comparison: Sundarpur and Kesarganj as treatment blocks, and Nandgaon block — matched on ASER 2024 reading level, UDISE+ pupil-teacher ratio and proportion of two-teacher schools — as a comparison block with no *Buniyaad* activity. Sample: 1,800 children per round, stratified by block and grade. The contract requires publication of the end-line report regardless of findings.

**Data protection.** Written consent is taken from the parent or guardian for every child assessed, on a Hindi-language form countersigned by the head teacher; consent is refusable without consequence. Child records are stored against a project-generated pseudonymous ID; names sit in a separate access-controlled table available only to the Assessment and Data Officer and the Project Manager. All external reporting, including to the funder, uses aggregated and anonymised data only. No child photographs are collected without separate specific written consent. Child-level data is retained for five years and then irreversibly deleted, with the deletion log reviewed at annual audit. Data is held on servers located in India.

## Feasibility

The three-year design is itself a staged feasibility test, not a single leap to full scale. Year 1 establishes the model with PSF staff facilitating directly; Year 2 introduces co-facilitation by government Cluster Resource Coordinators; Year 3 tests whether gains hold as CRCs lead at least 70 per cent of circles unaided and Learning Facilitator caseload rises from 15 to 22 schools. The specific threshold that would trigger a costed scale-up proposal for the remaining six blocks of Rampur (see Sustainability and Exit) is: Grade 3 reading at or above 50 per cent under the lighter, CRC-led Year 3 model, confirmed by the independent end-line. Falling short of that threshold under CRC-led delivery would mean re-costing the model with a higher PSF staffing ratio before any scale-up is proposed, rather than scaling the Year 1-2 staffing-heavy version as-is.

## Child Protection and Safeguarding

PSF operates the **Prayaas Child Protection and Safeguarding Policy**, adopted by the Governing Board on 14 March 2022 and last reviewed on 9 April 2025. It is compliant with the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act, 2012 and the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015.

- **Vetting.** Every staff member, intern, consultant and volunteer with child contact undergoes police verification through the district police office before deployment; no unaccompanied child contact is permitted until clearance is received. Two written references are checked.
- **Training.** All child-facing staff complete a full-day safeguarding induction before first deployment and a half-day refresher every six months. Attendance is recorded and reported to the Board annually.
- **Behaviour rules.** A signed code of conduct prohibits one-to-one unobserved contact with a child, private transport of children, exchange of personal contact details, and photography without written consent.

## Sustainability and Exit

The exit strategy is transfer of function, not transfer of funding.

**Year 1** — PSF Block Coordinators facilitate all cluster circles; the model is documented and costed.
**Year 2** — Cluster Resource Coordinators co-facilitate; the SCERT-reviewed handbook is positioned as a system resource; the first institutionalisation workshop presents mid-line evidence and a per-block cost of extension to the District Education Office.
**Year 3** — CRCs lead at least 70 per cent of circles unaided; Learning Facilitator load rises from 15 to 22 schools, deliberately reducing intensity to test whether gains hold under a lighter, government-led model. The end-line evaluation reports on both.

At close PSF hands over the SCERT-reviewed handbook and card set with print-ready files and no licensing restriction, the assessment instrument and scoring guide, a costed scale-up note for the remaining six blocks of Rampur, and the full anonymised dataset. PSF's presence in the two blocks ends at Month 36. Continuation, if any, would be through Samagra Shiksha or district funds, not a renewed grant for the same schools.
